# Danish coin rates 1481–1513: Gulden, Lübeck money, Hvid, Skilling, and the silver they imply

> **Status:** internal research dossier (role-1), opened 2026-10-17 at the curator's request («збери всі можливі
> дані про курс данських монет до будь-чого іншого … за кожен рік … можливо вдасться визначити роки монет
> які мають вагу/пробу але недатовані … чи був перехід вслід за любеком»). Feeds `luebischer_muenzfuss.md`
> (§3.4b–g, the card stages) and `mark_lybsk_mark_dansk.md` (§4).
>
> **Question:** (1) every dated rate of Danish money against a yardstick whose silver (or gold) is known —
> the Rhenish Gulden, the Lübeck mark / Schilling / Penning, Swedish money, the Nobel; (2) the fine silver per
> Hvid and per Skilling that each rate implies; (3) whether the assayed, undated Hans coins fall into groups
> that the dated rates can place in time.
>
> **Excluded, and where it lives:** the Lübeck standard itself (`luebischer_muenzfuss.md` §3.1, §3.4g); the
> Gylden's own gold standard (`rhinsk_gylden_fod.md`); Christian II after Aug 1513 (`instruments/1514_moentordning_malmoe.md`);
> Norway's own reckoning (survey `hans_silver_1481_1513.md` §10.4). Sterling and the 1512 Lübeck execution case: curator
> said leave aside (2026-10-16).

## §2. The yardstick and its silver

The Gulden is the only yardstick that recurs year by year. Its silver value is not printed in any Danish
document; it is taken from Lübeck, where both the Gulden's price and the Schilling's silver are known.

Recomputed: Gulden in silver = 24 × Lübeck ß fine. **From c. 1470 to 1506 the Gulden stood at 24 ß lübisch in
the market; what changed in 1506 was the Lübeck Schilling's silver (1,271 → 1,124 g), so the Gulden's silver
equivalent fell from ≈ 30,5 to ≈ 27 g.** ⚠ Wilcke 7-1 s. 142 has «En rhinsk Gylden var lig 20 ß lybsk og 2 M
dansk, senere 24 ß lybsk og 2½ M dansk» — Jesse's market price is 24 ß from 1470; the 20 ß is Lübeck's
1432 figure. Wilcke gives no date or document for his 20. R4 (after Enemark) has 1½ M lübisch = 24 ß already for
the period «bis etwa 1500». We follow R3/R4: the Gulden in Lübeck money stayed at 24 ß; the 2 → 2½ step is
on the Danish side only.

**Assumption used below (labelled):** the Gulden passed in Denmark at the same silver value as in Lübeck. ~~No
source states it~~ (corrected 2026-10-17, see §2a: the Danish market priced the Gulden in Lübeck money at
Lübeck's own figure); Wilcke's own reading («2 M eller 2½ M d. = 1 r. Gylden regnes at ækvivalere ca. 29 gr.
Sølv», 7-1 s. 134) uses the same assumption. If the Danish market valued gold higher (a higher relation),
every implied Danish silver figure below rises in proportion.

### §2a. Did Denmark value the Gulden as Lübeck did? (2026-10-17, curator question)

| Evidence | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Ribe, 1501** (a Nuremberg-Lübeck merchant's slip in the Ribe/Kolding toll roll): «*Item 800 mr lub ist 533 gulden 8 ß lub den gulden to 2,5 mr ist 1333 mr 5 ß 1 wit denisch*» | On the Danish market the Gulden = 1½ M lübisch = **24 ß lübisch — Lübeck's own market price** (§2) — and = 2½ M dansk on the same slip. Direct attestation of the assumption for 1501 | R4 s. 234 (RAK Ribe og Kolding Toldregnskab 1501) |
| «*Bis etwa 1500 waren es noch ein rheinischer Gulden zu 1,5 lübischen Mark oder zwei dänischen Mark gewesen*» | same 24 ß lübisch per Gulden in Denmark before 1500 | R4 s. 234 after Enemark 2003 I s. 340 |
| 1474: Christian I's rents, tolls and dues «*auf 23 ß = 1 fl. gegründet*» | the Crown reckoned the Gulden in Lübeck ß at a Lübeck-level figure (23, while Lübeck's market had gone to 24) | R3 |
| 1487: 1 ß d. ≈ ¾ ß l. | with 32 ß d / G gives 24 ß l. / G | R1 s. 143 (Wilcke after himself) |
| Sweden, Hans' Møntanordning 4 Dec 1497: Gulden = «halffænnan» = **1½ M Stockholm** = 36 Ørtug = 28,08 g silver vs 2,47 g gold | **1 : 11,37** (Wilcke's computation); the Lübeck market relation 1477–92 was 1 : 11,51–12,06 | R1 s. 116, 127 (table) |
| Denmark, mint grid 1 Aug 1513 (Blicher): 2 M d = 32 ß of 0,875 g = 28 g silver per Gulden of 2,527 g gold | **1 : 11** — an official mint relation, not a market rate | R2 s. 152 («Forholdstallet mellem Guld og Sølv er herefter 1 : 11») |
| Denmark, Møntordning 1514: 24 ß of 0,997 g = 23,9 g per Gylden (18 kt, 72/M) | **1 : 9,9** — official, and per Wilcke a debasement | R2 s. 153–155 |

**Answer:** for the years the rates cover, the assumption is attested rather than assumed. In 1501 and «bis
etwa 1500» the Danish market priced the Gulden at exactly Lübeck's 24 ß lübisch. So the Gulden's silver in
Denmark = the silver of 24 Lübeck ß as they circulated there — the same 30,5 g (to 1506) as in Lübeck. No
source shows the Danish market valuing gold HIGHER than Lübeck: the neighbouring Swedish legal relation (1497,
1 : 11,37) and the Danish mint relations (1513 1 : 11, 1514 1 : 9,9) are LOWER. If the Danish figure differed,
it lowered the silver per Gulden, not raised it.

**Sensitivity of §6 (recomputed, Gulden gold 2,52 g):** relation 11,0 → 27,7 g; 11,37 → 28,7 g; 12,06 → 30,4 g.
- Hvid 6 lod (0,27–0,30) at 96 / G: needs 25,9–28,8 g — fits 11,0–11,4 and sits just under 12,06; unchanged.
- Hvid 5 lod (≈ 0,18–0,24) at 120 / G: needs 21,6–28,8 g — fits every relation; unchanged.
- Skilling 9 lod (1,24–1,27) at 24 / G: needs 29,8–30,5 g — **only at the Lübeck relation (≈ 12)**; at 11,0 it
  would be 22 per G. Since 1501 shows the Lübeck price in Denmark, the reading stands.
- Skilling 8 lod (0,99–1,24) at 24 / G: needs 23,8–29,8 g — fits 11,0–12,06 before 1506 as well as the 27,0 g
  after 1506. **So the 8-lod group can be either «c. 1500–1505 debased» or «after 1506, Lübeck's 13 Mark»; the
  relation does not decide.** Its date rests on the reckoning (5 Hvide at 2½ M d) only.
- Skilling 7 / 6 lod (≈ 0,7–1,0) at 32 / G: needs 22–32 g — unchanged.

Caveat: which Lübeck coins circulated in Ribe in 1501 (the 1468/92 Schillinge at 1,271 g, or older and worn
pieces) is not stated; the 24 ß figure is the Lübeck price, its silver is the Lübeck standard's.

⚠ The «*1 M dansk i Sverige, særlig Østgøtaland og Smaaland … = 18 Ørtuger eller ½ r. Gylden af Finvægt 14,0418
gr.*» (R1 s. 134) matches, in Wilcke's own table on s. 126–127, the row of the **M gutisk** (Gotland mark: 16 ß =
6 Øre = 18 Ørtug = 14,0418 g); the row «1 M dansk (Götaland)» there is 24 Ørtug = 18,7224 g. Hypothesis: s. 134
mislabels the Gotland mark as «M dansk». Would be settled by: Wilcke's Swedish source (Styffe / Wallroth).

### §2b. A silver-to-silver anchor: the Saxon coins in the queen's account (2026-10-17)

Independent of any gold : silver relation. Saxony's coins after 1498 / 1500 (R10, after von Langenn 1838):
Guldengroschen («Klappmützentaler», Leipziger Münzordnung 1500) «*zu 8 Stück auf die 15lötige Mark … 27,40g
Feinsilber*», = 1 Gulden = 7 Schreckenberger = 21 Zinsgroschen; Schreckenberger 3,9 g fine; Zinsgroschen («Schneeberger»)
88 per mark at 7½ lot 4 grän ≈ 1,28 g fine (recomputed).

| Entry | Price | If the coin is a Guldengroschen (27,40 g) | If it is the small coin of that name |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1507 «Sneberger» (R9 s. 246) | 132 Hvide | **Hvid ≈ 0,208 g** | Zinsgroschen 1,28 g → Hvid ≈ 0,010 g — impossible |
| 1511 «en hell Skreckenberg» (R9 s. 386) | 128 Hvide | **Hvid ≈ 0,214 g** | Schreckenberger 3,9 g → Hvid ≈ 0,030 g — impossible |

**Reading (hypothesis):** both prices only make sense for a whole Saxon Guldengroschen — struck at the Annaberg
(Schreckenberg) and Schneeberg mints, and a fitting godparent's gift. Then a Hvid in 1507–1511 carried ≈ 0,21 g of
silver at face value, i.e. 120 Hvide (2½ M d) ≈ 25 g per Gulden — in the range §2a gives (relation 11 → 27,7 g;
1514 grid 23,9 g), and exactly the **5-lod Hans Hvid** (≈ 0,18–0,24 g, §5). A coin bought as a gift may carry a
premium, so these are upper bounds on the Hvid's price, lower bounds on its silver. Would be settled by: the
names «Sneberger» / «Schreckenberg» for Guldengroschen in another Danish or Hanseatic source.

**Answer to «did Denmark follow Lübeck to 13 Mark?» on this evidence:** the 8-lod Skilling is the coin
the 13-Mark step predicts (24 per Gulden after 1506, priced at 5 Hvide at 2½ M d). The rates show the Danish
step to 2½ M d at c. 1500–1501 — **before** Lübeck's 1504/06 step — so the reckoning moved first and the
Skilling's silver may have followed either Lübeck or the market. Consistent, not proven (curator's position
`luebischer_muenzfuss.md` §3.4d unchanged).

## §7. Our data — what this would let us do

Our Hans silver (`danish_realm`, survey §4) is undated except the 1512 Skillinger. A coin with a measured
weight but no fineness cannot be placed by §6 (weights of the 9-, 8-, 7- and 6-lod Skillinger overlap,
2,13–2,65 g). Only assayed specimens can enter a group; those are Eriksson's, and he lists Galster type and
mint, not our seed ids. No data change proposed; this is a dating aid for phase descriptions (curator's call).
