# Danish denomination grids 1403–1914 — common and separate grids, transitions, the ratios behind them

> **Status:** research dossier (role-1), opened 2026-11-12 at the curator's request. The brief:
> - separate the common and the separate denomination grids through the whole period, not stopping at 1541;
> - cover every denomination of each period;
> - give the transition years and the ratio computations each conclusion rests on.
>
> First written to 1700; extended to 1914 the same day at the curator's request.
> **Scope:** which Danish (and Norwegian) denominations formed ONE grid and which stood in SEPARATE
> grids, period by period from 1403 to 1914; the year of each transition; the set ratios and the
> silver computations each conclusion rests on. The Danish : Lübeck tie is treated as a grid question;
> the duchies enter from 1788, when they form their own monetary area.
> **Excluded:** the full ratio chains per act to 1625 — `denomination_ratio_chains_1403_1625.md` (this
> dossier reads them and extends to 1700); the formula series of each line —
> `danish_small_coin_lines_1497_1660.md`; act-by-act parameters — `instruments/<yyyy>_*.md` (every grid
> figure below is taken from the §4 of the instrument dossier named, recomputed there); gold standards
> (only where gold is tariffed against a grid); paper money except where a set rate ties notes to coin;
> the duchies before 1788 (they follow the Lübeck chain, §9).
> «[C]» = computed here. Cologne mark 233,855 g. Fine silver per Skilling (g/ß) is the comparison unit
> for the metal test: the coin's fine weight divided by its value in ß as SET.

## 1. Criterion

- **One grid:** denominations linked to one base by **integer ratios set by an act** (or by the coin's
  own name where the act names the sort by value). The SET / GRID / obs. labels of
  `denomination_ratio_chains_1403_1625.md` apply.
- **Separate grids:** two sets of denominations each integer inside itself, joined to each other only by
  a ratio that is (a) not integer in the units of either, or (b) changes from act to act while each set
  stays fixed inside, or (c) is a market rate / agio with no act.
- **Metal test (supporting, never decisive):** inside one grid, g/ß falls smoothly from large to small
  coin (Scheidemünze gradient). A step that coincides with a floating tariff link marks the seam.
- The criterion is the curator's (2026-11-05: Fuß = formula + inertial continuity; units not sub-units
  of each other = two grids). Applying it to the material below is this dossier's work; each «grid»
  verdict is a reading of SET ratios, not a term found in the sources.

## 3a. Before 1424 — a period of its own? (curator question 2026-11-27)

Why «1403/1424»: 1403 is the start of the curator's research window and the first certain royal
production (Næstved sterlings, `danish_standard_chronology_1396_1541.md` §2a, M s. 379); 1424 is the
first act that sets a Danish chain. The double year mixed two criteria. The royal coinage itself
restarts c. 1400–1403 (Næstved Witten; first mention 1 Oct 1402 «*32 mark sølv i hvid, lovlig og
gængse Sjællands mønt*»); before that the Danish deeds of 1380–1410 reckon «hvide» = 4 Lübeck hollow
pence, i.e. on Lübeck coin (Hb s. 136 n. 2), and Margrete's forordning of 1396 names no coin.

**c. 1400/1403 – 1424, what the sources give (chronology §2a, chains §1):**
- coins: Witten / Hvid (Næstved, crown / crozier), crown hollow penny, Erik's sterling (Næstved,
  Lund, c. 1403–1412/13), copper «Kobbersterling» c. 1422 – Whitsun 1423; no Søsling, no Skilling coin;
- internal set: sterling = 3 Pf (M s. 381 «*sterling (=tre penninge)*»), Hvid = 4 Pf, ß = 12 — the
  Lübeck set 1-3-4-12 without the 6;
- tie to Lübeck: not par, not integer, contested. The towns complain 1403–1404 that the queen's
  officials pay the Danish coin out «*vor vul*» and refuse it «*vor vul*» (HR I 5 S. 107, 119, 124);
  the 1406 recess rates the Danish penny at 2 Pf l. and the Lübeck mark at 12 Danish ß (the editor:
  «*mir nicht verständlich*», ⚠ chains §1); 1410 the sterling (3 Pf) at 2 Lübeck Pf, the crown hollow
  penny at ½ (M s. 381). Level [C]: sterling 6,1–9,8 Mark per fine mark against Lübeck 5,4 (1406).

**8 Oct 1424 changes three things at once:**
1. the old Danish coin is re-based by half — «*old Danish 4-Pf pieces cut to 2 new Pf, crown hollow
   pennies to ½*» (Jesse via `mark_lybsk_mark_dansk.md` §4): a SET integer conversion (2 : 1), the
   names kept, the unit's value halved;
2. the Danish coin becomes the common coin with the four towns, «*in allen drei Reichen außer in Bergen
   gleichmäßig*» (Sechsling 168 / 11¼ lot, level 7,47 = Lübeck 7,47): the Danish lattice and the
   Lübeck lattice coincide for the first time;
3. the 6-Pf piece (Søsling) enters the Danish repertoire, completing the set 1-3-4-6-12.

**Reading [C, «можна вважати»].** By the internal criterion of §1 the two stretches are ONE grid: the
same set of units, and the 1424 conversion is integer (like the renaming of 1572, §5), not a floating
seam. By the Danish : Lübeck criterion of §9 they differ: before 1424 a separate Danish currency on
the Lübeck set, joined to Lübeck money at non-integer, disputed rates; from 1424 one coin with Lübeck
(the par soon lapsed — «*Nur die Anlehnung des dänischen Münzwesens an das der Städte blieb
bestehen*», Jesse — but no new internal seam opened). So **c. 1400/1403 – 1424 can be counted a period
of its own**: same grid, separated from the next by a re-basing (×½), by the change from an
incommensurable to a common tie with Lübeck, and by a new denomination. Whether to split §3 at 1424 is
the curator's call; the start would then be c. 1400–1403 (royal coinage), not 1403 as a fixed year.

Open: the 1406 recess's internal contradiction (Danish penny 2 Pf l. but Lübeck mark = 12 Danish ß)
— settled by the HR I 5 Nr. 310 text and assays read afresh; the value of the copper sterling (1 Pf,
Hb s. 170, or 3 Pf, M s. 385).

- 1524: Mark piece 9,301 g = 11,95 four-Hvid ß; Sølvgylden 25,58 g = 32,9 four-Hvid ß against the

loose (30 such ß = 2½ M).

assigned at all (¾ Hvid non-integer); Penning → B (12 per ß; 3 or 4 per Hvid floats); gros → none (a
tariffed episode); Skærv → no tariff known.

Curator question: when exactly does the one grid of §3 become the two of §4, and is grid A or B the
continuation of §3, or are both separate from it.

Dating of Hans' first Skilling (`hans_rates_1481_1513.md` §3): the Gribshunden purse (June 1495) holds
Hvide only («*may indicate that these nominals were not minted before June 1495, but the sample size …
is too small*»); the Møllegården hoard, 190 Skillinger of Hans, «*may indicate … that skilling also was
issued late in the 1490s*»; the first rate «*4 Hvide ß, som Kong Hans mønte lod*» is of 1497 (Wilcke 7-2
s. 142). The rates 1483–1495 show one Danish level only — G = 2 M d. (1483, 1485 «*12 og 20 ß*» in
Hvide, 1487, «bis etwa 1500»), Hvid = 3 Pf lübisch (1495) — which at Gulden silver ≈ 30,5 g give the
same Hvid, 0,318 g (hans_rates §4, recomputed).

**When.** Inside the realm the split begins when a Skilling coin is again struck and passes at more
than 3 Hvide: **c. 1495–1497**. 1481 (accession of Hans) is a change of reign, not of grid: 1481–1495
the Danish coin is the Hvid and the reckoning is the Hvid one, as under Christian I. The split is
complete c. 1500–1501, when the Gulden moves from 2 to 2½ M d. (= 96 → 120 Hvide) while staying 24 ß
in the Skilling count (hans_rates §3; `mark_lybsk_mark_dansk.md` §4). The boundary of §3 / §4 is
therefore set at c. 1495/97 (it was written «c. 1481» in the first version of this dossier).

**Which branch continues the trunk.**
- **Grid A is the trunk.** The Hvid is the one denomination struck without a break from the 14th c. to
  1513, with a gradual fall of fine weight (0,328 → 0,267 → 0,206 g) — the inertial continuity of the
  same denomination that the curator's definition of a Fuß requires (`danish_small_coin_lines_1497_1660.md`
  §1). The account of grid A is the trunk's own: ß dansk = 3 Hvide, Mark dansk = 16 ß dansk = 48 Hvide
  (Wilcke 7-2 s. 149 «*M dansk à 16 ß à 3 Hvide*»), and under Christian I, with no Skilling coin struck,
  the realm already reckoned only in this Hvid mark (G = 2 M d., Viken rent). From 1448 the trunk is,
  in practice, grid A.
- **Grid B is not a continuation of the trunk's Skilling coin.** (a) Fifty years without a Danish
  Skilling (1448 → c. 1497). (b) Hans' Skilling starts heavier than the last trunk Skilling (≈ 1,27 g
  against 0,961 g of 1448) — at the level of the Lübeck Schilling of 1468/92 (1,271 g, hans_rates §4),
  not on the trunk's falling line. (c) It is counted in the Lübeck mark: Wilcke 7-2 s. 149 «*delt
  Valutaen i M lybsk à 16 ß lybske eller danske Skillinger og M dansk à 16 ß à 3 Hvide*» («divided the
  currency into the Mark lybsk of 16 Lübeck or Danish ß and the Mark dansk of 16 ß of 3 Hvide»). Grid B
  is a new line pegged to the Lübeck Schilling, joined to the Danish Hvid at a floating rate. The
  Søsling follows it (½ ß, struck again from 1497 after a 55-year gap).
- **What B does inherit — hypothesis.** The trunk's ß-to-Gulden relation: Sjælland 1449 «*1 Rhinsk
  Gylden skal regnes til 24 Skilling gode Sølvpenge*» (hans_rates §3, R6) and the 1514 act's 24 ß per
  Gylden are the same count, so B may revive the trunk's 1440s Skilling-Gylden link (24) rather than its
  coin. Not shown: whether the «gode Sølvpenge» of 1449 are Christoffer's / the Rigsråd's Skillinger or
  Lübeck money. Would be settled by the 1449 source (R6 in hans_rates) read in full.
  - **The 1449 source read (2026-11-27) — the hypothesis loses its only support as a Danish rate, and
    stays open.** The item is a private pledge deed, not an act: København, Palm Sunday 6 April 1449,
    Anders Troelssøn of Næstved owes **50 Mark lybsk** «*i gode Sølvpenge eller rynsk Guld at betale og
    Gyldenen for 24 Skill., om nogen Kobbermønt eller anden argere Mønt blev gænge i Siæland efter denne
    Tid*» (Rep. dipl. 1. R. III s. 759, nr. 7877, a regest; page image read; full row in hans_rates §3).
    What it shows: the 24 Skilling per Gylden are counted in the reckoning of the debt, Mark lybsk
    (G = 1½ M lybsk), and «gode Sølvpenge» is good silver coin as opposed to copper or worse coin — no
    coin type, mint or issuer is named. What it does not show: whether the silver handed over would be
    Lübeck Schillinge or Danish Skillinger struck to that reckoning. So the deed attests a 24-ß Gylden
    in lybsk money of account in Sjælland in 1449, between two private parties; it is not evidence of a
    Danish Skilling coin tariffed at 24 per Gylden. Would settle it: the full text of the deed (RA,
    «Ringsted H. 94»; a copy in Langebeks Diplomatarium) — only if it names the coin; or a Lübeck
    Gulden rate for 1449 to compare with 24 ß (not looked up).
- **The 1513–14 contact is a return of B to the trunk's ratio,** not a third grid: the Blicher letter
  sets ß = 3 Hvide again («*Forholdet til den lybske ß ophævet*», Wilcke s. 151), and the 1514 grid puts
  ß 0,997 g = 3,00 Hvide (0,332) — the trunk relation restored for one act.

**Answer in one line:** the one grid of §3 narrows to the Hvid and its account under Christian I
(1448 – c. 1497) and continues as grid A; grid B is a separate line opened by Hans' Skilling c. 1495–97,
pegged to Lübeck, inheriting at most the trunk's 24-ß Gylden count.

## 8b. 1788 – 1813: the duchies' own grid; specie and kurant set at 4 : 5 in the kingdom

## 8c. 1813 – 1873: Rigsbankdaler; duchies on two grids; Norway back to the specie

Pattern [reading, not a source term]: three times a large-coin unit and a small-coin unit run on separate
grids joined by a floating link (Gylden / Mark dansk against ß, 1497–1536; Rdl against ß, 1602–1619;
specie Rdl against kurant, 1647–1794), and each time an act restores one integer chain (1537/41,
1619/25, 1794). The kurant period adds a fourth seam inside the coin of account (Krone 17 : 16 against
kurant, 1737–71). After 1794 the seams are no longer between denominations of one realm but between
monetary areas (duchies 1788–1864, Norway from 1814) and between coin and paper (1813–45); the decimal
Krone of 1873/75 closes the last of them.
