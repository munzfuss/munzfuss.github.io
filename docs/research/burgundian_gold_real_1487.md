# The great gold real of 1487 (groote gouden reaal / grand réal d'or): research dossier

> **Status:** internal research dossier (role-1). Created 2026-10-11.
>
> **Question this dossier settles:** what the sources document about the gold
> standard of the Burgundian-Habsburg Netherlands of 1487, which Galster names as
> the model of the Danish Nobel. It covers the instruments that set it, the
> parameters of the real and its parts (Bourgondische nobel, dukaat/florijn), the
> tariff values, where it was struck, and who reproduces whom. It also covers how
> these figures sit against the Phase-0 row of our Nobelfod card.
>
> **Excluded, and where it lives:**
> - The Danish Nobel itself (Hans 1496 → Frederik I 1532, the 1514/1524 ordinances)
>   is in `docs/research/nobel_fod.md`. This dossier only feeds its §3.1 (period 0).
> - The silver side of the 1487 ordinance is out of scope. That covers the silver
>   real, the double and single griffioen and the black money; their parameters are
>   recorded in §3.4 only as transcribed context. Not written up anywhere else.
> - The Andriesgulden / Bourgondische gulden and the 1489 «halve nobel / schuitken»
>   are out of scope. Van Gelder 1995 pp. 52–53 covers them if ever needed.
> - Market prices and single specimens are out of scope (§7a). Specimen weights
>   appear in §5 only as a check.
>
> **Sources read for this dossier** (local copies in the session scratchpad;
> nothing cached in `scripts/cache/`):

**Denomination ratios** (consistent across every valuation): real = 2 Bourgondische
nobel = 4 dukaat / florijn. Van Gelder 1995 p. 53: «*de onderdelen heetten
Bourgondische nobel en dukaat, maar die namen komen zelden voor*». Note: the Dutch
«Bourgondische nobel» is the HALF real (≈ 7.4 g). The Danish Nobel corresponds in
weight to the WHOLE real. The shared word «nobel» does not mean the same coin.

**Who struck it.**
- Van Gelder 1951 p. 46: «*Goud werd dan ook practisch niet geslagen; alleen te
  Dordrecht had een niet omvangrijke aanmunting plaats, terwijl elders slechts enkele
  stukken vermoedelijk voor representatieve doeleinden vervaardigd zijn*».
- Van Gelder 1995 p. 32 calls the reals representative pieces: «*grote representatieve
  gouden en zilveren realen (eenmaal genoemd "penning van toog en parament", d.w.z.
  munt van pronk en opschik)*».
- Van Gelder 1951's Overzicht (p. 51) lists the gold real for Vlaanderen (Deschamps de
  Pas 15,15, «1487») and Holland (Van der Chijs pl. 17,1, «1487»).
- For Gelre it is doubtful (n. 18, p. 53): «*Het bestaan van gouden en zilveren reaal
  van Gelre is twijfelachtig*».
- Galster's «*Dordrechts Møntmærke*» on the Danish pieces fits the Holland (Dordrecht)
  issue being the model.

**Mint quota** (De Witte II p. 74), April instruction: «*De chaque masse de 20 marcs
d'or à travailler, le maître devait livrer: un marc de Réaux, 2 marcs de Nobles et 17
marcs de Florins*». So the real was 5 % of the gold by weight, by design.

### §2a. 1488 → 1514: was the real standard changed again? (checked 2026-10-11)

**Result.** No later instrument that changes the pieces-per-mark or the fineness of
the great gold real has been found. What is attested is (a) a possible general lowering
in July 1488 whose application to the gold real is open, (b) the end of striking in
1488, and (c) the real surviving only as a valued tariff coin at the 16½ standard.

|---|---|---|

| **July 1488** | Instruction lowers «weight and fineness of all pieces» by ≈ 10 %. It is not stated that the gold real is included; the text of the instruction is not seen | Van Gelder 1951 p. 47: «*een instructie van Juli 1488 verlaagde gewicht en gehalte van alle stukken met ongeveer 10 %*» (n. 9: Verachter p. 41, not seen) |

Silver real/griffioen are outside the scope (§0).

**What this does and does not settle.**
- The Danish Nobel (1496–1532) was struck while the Dutch real was only a valued tariff
  coin at 16½ to the mark. The model's *parameters* did not change in that time on the
  evidence above; its *striking* had stopped in 1488.
- Whether the July 1488 instruction touched the gold real remains open (Q3): Van Gelder
  1995 keeps 14.72 g in 1488, but the tariff he cites (20 May 1488) is earlier than the
  instruction.
- Holland and Flanders instructions are not covered by De Witte (Brabant). The Holland
  account (Van der Chijs 1858 p. 491) was not seen (Q2).

## §3. Parameters

### §3.1 Mark and carat conventions

- **Mark.** Vanhoudt p. xxiv: «*De ordonnanties drukten de massa (M) van de munten …
  uit onder de vorm van het aantal stukken (de snede) dat moest worden geslagen uit 1
  Trooise mark van ca. 244,75 gram, met uitzondering van de munten van Willem I, waarvan
  de massa werd uitgedrukt in Hollandse azen, i.e. ¹⁄₅₁₂₀ deel van de iets zwaardere
  Hollandse mark van ca. 246,08 gram*».
  → For a 1487 ordinance the mark is the **Trooise mark ≈ 244.75 g**. «Troy» = Troyes:
  the Dutch «Trooise mark», French «marc de Troyes» and Danish «troysk Mark» name the
  same unit. 246.08 g is the later, heavier **Hollandse** variant.
- **Carat.** Vanhoudt p. xxiv: «*De ordonnanties preciseerden het goudgehalte in
  karaat (zuiver goud = 24 karaat) en greinen (1 karaat = 12 grein); in sommige gevallen
  werd zuiver goud gelijkgesteld aan de Engelse gouden nobel, met een reëel gehalte van
  23 karaat 10 ½ grein, hetzij ca. 995‰*». His table books «24 kt» as 995 ‰.
  → **Hypothesis (not verified for 1487):** the instruction's «XXIV carats» is
  noble-standard «24 carat» ≈ 23 kt 10½ gn (.99479), not chemically pure gold. Support:
  Van Gelder's computed fine content and Numista's .992 (§3.2) both sit below 1.000.
  Settled by: the instruction text with its remedium, or an assay (Q1).

### §3.1a. What «24 carats» means in Burgundian-Habsburg instruments (checked 2026-10-11)

**Result.** In the Netherlands ordinances «fine gold» was a *conventional* standard, not
chemically pure gold. Three instruments in De Witte II say so in their own words; two
later ones state the same standard numerically. Nothing found states a 1487 instruction
using the formula, so the reading for the real itself remains an inference from
consistent usage 1469 → 1521 (Q1 stays open for 1487).

| Year / instrument | Wording (De Witte II, OCR text; carat-grain = 1/12 carat unless noted) | Page |
|---|---|---|
| 1469, Bruges conference, Charles the Bold and Edward IV | «*l'on considérerait comme or pur le nouveau noble anglais, appelé royal, ouvré à [X]XIII carats III grains et demi*» — the English noble counted as pure gold (23 ct 3½ gr in English quarter-carat grains = 23.875 ct = .9948) | 39–40 |
| Burgundian florin (Philip the Good → Charles) | «*à XIX carats, le noble Henricus compté pour fin*» | 44, 53 |
| **Instruction after the ordinance of 14 Dec 1489** | «*double Florin au Saint-André; c'est-à-dire à XXIV carats — le noble Henricus compté pour fin*» — **24 carats is explicitly the noble counted as fine** | 79 |
| Instruction of 20 Feb 1500 | Toison d'or «*XXIII carats IX 1/2 grains*», 54½ to the mark | 116–117 |
| Ordinance of 4 Feb 1521 (Charles V) | «*ung denier de fin or qui se nommera real et contiendra vingt trois caratz neuf grains et demy d'or fin*», 46 to the mark; merchants' «*matières de fin or*» are paid by assay above 23 ct 9½ gr | 160 |

Cross-check (arithmetic): 244.75 / 54.5 = 4.491 g × (23 + 9.5/12)/24 = 4.452 g = Van Gelder
1995's «4.49 g / 4.45 g fijn» for the Golden-Fleece florin (p. 53 list). The same list
gives the rijder 3.63 / 3.60 g (.9917), the Hungarian ducat 3.55 / 3.52 g (.9915) and the
real 14.83 / 14.72 g (.9926): Van Gelder's computed fine contents for the «24-carat»
pieces cluster at .991–.993, never at 1.000.

**Who noticed.** Vanhoudt 2015 p. xxiv states the convention generally («*in sommige
gevallen werd zuiver goud gelijkgesteld aan de Engelse gouden nobel … 23 karaat 10 ½
grein, hetzij ca. 995‰*»). De Witte reproduces the contemporary wording «*compté pour
fin*». Van Gelder computes the net contents without commenting on the discrepancy.
Not found: an assay study of a Dutch gold real. Outside the Netherlands, Munro's
Toronto lecture notes call the Venetian ducat «*virtually 24 carats*» and give the
English noble as .995 (Table 3); a figure of 99.7 % for florin and ducat attributed
to Spufford turned up only in a search summary and is **not verified** here.

**Consequence for the Nobelfod card.** The number «24 carats» can be reported as what the
instruments call the standard; it must not be converted to 1.000 or presented as the
Danish Nobel's fineness. Whether the 1487 real's «XXIV carats» meant the same noble
standard is supported by usage in 1469, 1489 and 1521 but not stated for 1487.

### §3.4 Silver side (context only, not analysed)

As transcribed by De Witte II pp. 73–74 (page scan): silver real «XI deniers IV grains», 35

## §4. Recomputation

- **Mass.** 244.75 / 16.5 = 14.8333 → Van Gelder's 14.83 g ✓ (legal mass). With the
  Hollandse mark: 246.084 / 16.5 = 14.914. With Galster's stated weight: «*16 1/2 paa
  en troysk Mark, d. v. s. den skulde veje 14,9g*» → 14.9 × 16.5 = 245.85 g. So
  **Galster computed on the heavier (~246 g) mark**, and his 14,9 g is 0.5 % above the
  legal mass that Van Gelder takes from the instructions.
- **Fineness implied by Van Gelder.** 14.72 / 14.83 = .99258 = 23 kt 9.86 gn. This is
  not a round carat value:
  - 23 kt 10 gn = .99306 → 14.727 g;
  - 23 kt 10½ gn = .99479 → 14.756 g;
  - 24 kt → 14.833 g.
  Van Gelder's 14.72 is the nearest to 23 kt 10 gn of these (Δ −0.05 %). It cannot come
  from 24/24. → He used a prescribed fineness below 24 kt that this dossier has not seen
  (Q1).
- **Money of account.** 14.72 g / 192 st × 20 = 1.533 g gold per gulden of 20 st →
  Van Gelder 1995 table I p. 35 «*1487 (sep.) grote reaal 14,72 192 1,53 51*» ✓.
  For 1488: 14.72 / 240 × 20 = 1.227 → «1,23» ✓.
- **De Witte's December rates.**
  - 32 sous de gros × 12 = 384 groot = 192 stuiver → 9 Sept valuation 192 ✓.
  - 36 sous = 216 stuiver → 20 Dec valuation 216 ✓.
  - Nobel 16 → 18 sous = 96 → 108 st ✓.
  - Florin 8 → 9 sous = 48 → 54 st ✓.

## §6. Against our data (Nobelfod card, Phase 0)

`data/shared/fuesse.yml::nobel_fod.grundwerte.rows[0]` (Phase 0, 1496–1513) currently
reads:
- «16½ Nobel per rough troy mark (Dutch, **246.084 g**)», refs
  `wikipedia-troy-weight-dutch-mark` + `galster-nobel-guldreal-24-karat`;
- «Rough weight **14.914 g** (= 246.084 / 16½)»;
- fineness unknown, with the note «the Dutch model had 24 carats».

Findings:
1. **Wrong mark for 1487 (per Vanhoudt).** The 1487 instruction counts pieces to the
   Trooise mark ≈ 244.75 g. The 246.084 g Wikipedia value is the Hollandse mark, which
   Vanhoudt reserves for Willem I. On 244.75 g the rough weight is **14.833 g**. That
   equals the legal mass in Van Gelder 1995 p. 53; 14.914 g is attested by no source
   except Galster's rounded «14,9». Proposed fix: mark 244.75 g, ref → Vanhoudt
   p. xxiv (new pool key). **Applied 2026-10-11** (curator «так»): mark 244.75 g, rough
   14.833 g, `soll_rau_by_phase['0']` 14.83333 / 29.66667 / 44.5, ref
   `vanhoudt-2015-trooise-mark`.
2. **«24 carats».** De Witte's instruction says XXIV carats, so the note «the Dutch
   model had 24 carats» is backed by the instrument, not just by Galster. Its ‰ value is
   open (Q1), so the card should not convert it to 1.000.
3. **Proposed new Grundwerte section** «Ordonnantie Maximilian 20 April 1487 /
   instructie juli 1487». Placement: between Phase 0 and «Møntordning Christian II 1514
