# The Danish small-coin lines 1497–1660 — Hvid, Skilling, Mark, Gylden / Daler: formula continuity and where the Lybsk system ends

> **Status:** research dossier (role-1), opened 2026-11-05. Feeds the end-point question of the
> `luebischer_muenzfuss` card (Lybsk Møntfod) and the placement of the Count's Feud coins
> (`christian_ii_partisans_coinage_1525_1536.md`).
> **Scope:** the formula (pieces per Cologne mark × fineness → fine silver) of each Danish
> denomination LINE from Hans to Frederik III, and the tie of the Danish units to the Lübeck units.
> **Excluded:** the chain of tariffs set by the acts — `denomination_ratio_chains_1403_1625.md`;
> Hans' assays in detail — `luebischer_muenzfuss.md` §0.6–§0.7; the duchies' Lübeck coin —
> `luebischer_muenzfuss.md` §3.15.
> Cologne mark 233,855 g; «[C]» = computed here from the cited grid.

## 1. The curator's definitions (2026-11-05)

- **A Fuß is defined by its formula AND by the inertial continuity of earlier standards in the same
  denominations.** Example given: N per Cologne mark and fineness 100 / .8, 105 / .8, 110 / .8,
  110 / .75, 120 / .7, 110 / .7, 110 / .6, 120 / .6 — one continuity. This holds for the SAME
  denominations (Skilling, Hvid). A new denomination with its own formula (e.g. the Daler) is examined
  separately: does it fit the existing system or form one of its own.
- **«Lybsk Møntfod» means the Danish standard of c. 1350–1514+** that oriented itself on the Lübeck
  coin without always reaching it, or deliberately struck worse — not the coin of Lübeck itself.
- **The Mark was strictly counted from the Hvid; the two are one system.**
- **The Skilling line: open** (whether the war collapse of 1534–36 still counts as continuity).

The 1514 Skilling continues Hans' last formula unchanged; 1524 lowers it a step; the war of 1534–36
drops it to the level of a Hvid (−75 %), and the state itself halves the war coin in 1537. Whether that
collapse is continuity is the curator's open point (§1).

## 4. New denominations — do they fit the system?

**The Mark (coin from 1524).** Mark piece 9,301 g fine = 47,7 Hvide (1524) [C]; 8,830 g = 48,0 Hvide
(1541) [C]. The Mark dansk of account was 48 Hvide already under Hans («*M dansk à 16 ß à 3 Hvide*»,
Wilcke 7-2 s. 149). The Mark coin is a coin for an existing unit — **fits** (curator: «Марка строго
рахувалась від гвіда, тому вони в одній системі»). 2-Mark line: 18,6 g (1524) → Christian III's
Klippinge 9,1 / 7,3 / 6,4 g, Count Christoffer's 4,8 g (1534–36, Wilcke 7-3 s. 242) → 2 × 8,83 g (1541).

**The Sølvgylden / Daler.**

| Years | Formula | Fine g | Tie to the small coin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sølvgylden 1514 | 8½/M, 14 lod | 24,07 | = 72 Hvide = 24 ß (Wilcke 7-2 s. 154) |
| Sølvgylden 1524 | 8/M, 14 lod | 25,58 | 2,75 Mark by silver against 2½ by tariff [C] |
| Daler 1541 | 8/M, 14½ lod | 26,49 | = 3 Mark (8,83) = 48 ß (0,551) = 144 Hvide (0,184) [C, Hede] |

One continuity among themselves; their formula (14–14½ lod) is not the small coin's (4–6 lod). In
1514 and 1541 the ordinance puts large and small coin on one silver level; in 1524 it does not.

The Danish units stay reckoned against the Lübeck ones through the 17th century; only the factor
changes (3 : 5 → 1 : 2; the 1572 attempt at 1 : 1 failed).

## 6. End-point candidates for the Lybsk Møntfod (curator decision pending)

By formula and continuity the Mark–Skilling–Hvid system does not end in 1541 or within the 16th
century; it passes into the Danish Mark–Skilling reckoning tied 1 : 2 to Lübeck. The end therefore
needs a further criterion:

- **(a) 1514** — the first ordinance grid on one silver level for large and small coin (the current
  end of the status layer, `luebischer_muenzfuss.md` §3.14). Then 1533–40 is a return to a regime
  without a grid, and from 1541 the small coin is a fraction of the Daler.
- **(b) 1537–1541** — the first integer chain anchored on the Daler (Gylden / Joachimsdaler = 3 Mark,
  Mark = 16 ß, ß = 12 Pf), see `denomination_ratio_chains_1403_1625.md`; «*i dansk mønt, ikke lybsk*»
  1541. The war coin of 1534–36 (both sides) falls inside the Lybsk Møntfod.
- **(c) the end of the Lübeck tie** — none: the ratio is fixed at least to Christian IV.
- **(d) c. 1619** — the last Hvid, the end of the line from which the Mark was counted.
