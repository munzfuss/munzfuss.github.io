# Denomination ratio chains, Denmark (and Norway), c. 1403 – 1625

> **Status:** research dossier (role-1), opened 2026-11-05 at the curator's request: «з 1403 по
> 1619+, співвідношення номіналів … почати з найменшого і далі до вищого … чи є місця де
> співвідношення не ціле і точки зміни співвідношень … які номінали були з якими в одній системі»,
> then «дивитись по заданих значеннях всяких ордонансів».
> **Scope:** the chain smallest → largest denomination (and gold where an act fixes it) as the
> normative acts SET it, Denmark (Norway where it differs; duchies only by note), c. 1403–1625.
> **Excluded:** assays and specimen weights (period dossiers); the formula series per line —
> `danish_small_coin_lines_1497_1660.md`; the Lübeck chain itself —
> `luebeck_standard_chronology_1226_1622.md`.
> Compiled from the instrument dossiers and the dossiers named below, with the primary text
> re-opened where a dossier was unclear (subagent pass 2026-11-05, key quotes re-checked by hand:
> Secher III nr. 173 / 208 s. 182, Secher I nr. 609, Wilcke 7-2 s. 174–175, RL 1397–1513 nr. 11).

**Rule applied (curator clarification).** A chain is built ONLY from values SET by a normative act:
Møntordning, Forordning, Aabent Brev, Kongebrev, Reces, Valvation, Bestalling, Hanseatic recess or
treaty. Three labels:
- **SET**: the act states the ratio.
- **GRID**: the act gives only pieces per mark and fineness. The ratio is *computed from the act's grid*
  (fine g ÷ fine g, at 233,855 g), so it is our arithmetic and not a tariff.
- **obs.**: a market, account or debt-letter rate, or an author's statement with no act. These rates are
  **observed, not set**. They are listed as secondary notes and never used in a chain.

A period with no act is marked **«no act»**. Abbreviations: Pf = Penning, ß = Skilling, M = Mark,
G = Gylden, Dlr/Rdl = (Rigs)daler, l. = lybsk, d. = dansk. *acc.* = unit of account; *coin* = struck
piece. The main sources are the dossiers in `docs/research/` (instrument dossiers, `danish_standard_chronology_1396_1541.md`,
`mark_lybsk_mark_dansk.md`, `hans_rates_1481_1513.md`, `ordinances_register.md`) and the works they quote:
Wilcke 1950 ("W") and 1919 ("W I"), Secher's *Corpus constitutionum Daniae* ("Secher"), Rigslovgivning ("RL").

## 1. Before 1424 (Margrete, Erik VII)

**Chain: no Danish act sets one.** Margrete's forordning, Viborg 24 Jan 1396, decides on a royal
coinage but names no standard, coin or unit (Kroman nr. 35–36 § 16). Hanseatic acts set two rates:

| link | ratio | status | source + quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Danish penny (sterling) : Lübeck Pf | 1 = 2 Pf l. | SET (Hanseatic recess, 31 Mar 1406) | HR I 5 Nr. 310, S. 230–231: «*de penningh 2 penninghe*» |
| Danish money : Lübeck money | 12 ß d. per Lübeck Mark (16 ß) | SET (same recess) | ibid.: «*so is de Lubesche mark nicht beter den 12 schillinge*» |
| Stralsund hollow penny : Lübeck Pf | 3 = 2 | SET (Môtbok, Margrete/Erik redaction [1407–12], art. 54; not a Danish coin) | RL 1397–1513 nr. 55 s. 198: «*dre sundesche hole penninghe vor twe lub.*» |

Observed, not set: the sterling was valued at 3 Pf in the realm and the crown hollow penny at ½ Pf l.
(Märcher s. 381, 1410). Deeds from 1380–1410 reckon a «hvid» at 4 Lübeck hollow pence (Hauberg s. 136 n. 2).
**Non-integer:** the 1406 recess makes the Lübeck Mark worth 12 ß of Danish money, so 1 Danish ß = 1⅓ ß l.
The editor of the recess calls the assays «*mir nicht verständlich*» (see the chronology §2a).
**Struck (outcomes from assays):** Hvid/Witten (Næstved, rare), sterling (3 Pf), crown hollow penny.
From c. 1422–23 Erik struck copper sterlings.

## 2. 8 Oct 1424 – c. 1481 (the common coin; Christopher; Christian I)

**Chain (8 Oct 1424 recess, coin names = Lübeck reckoning):** 1 Hohlpfennig = 1 Pf → 1 Sechsling = 6 Pf. This coin
was common to Denmark and the four towns, so the Danish money equalled the Lübeck money. The recess sets
no Mark, ß or Gylden link.

## 3. 1481 – July 1513 (Rigsråd, Hans)

**Chain: no act.** Hans's Håndfæstning of 1 Feb 1483 (Norway clause) binds Norway's coin to the Danish
one: «*jeffngod … wid Danske penninge*». His Môtbok of 11 Sep 1484, § 67, has a par clause: «*schal unse koninglyke munthe hir
gan gelyk der veerleye munte*». It also lists gold without values. Neither act sets a denomination link.
Wilcke 7-1 s. 128 writes: «*I Danmark synes netop ingen Møntordning at være udstedt*».

Observed, not set (`hans_rates_1481_1513.md`; W 7-2 s. 149):
- M d. = 16 ß à 3 Hvide (Wilcke).
- G = 2 M d. = 96 Hvide to c. 1500, then 2½ M d. = 120 Hvide. The Ribe toll roll of 1501 reads
  «*den gulden to 2,5 mr*».
- The Skilling coin = 4 Hvide (1497, «*4 Hvide ß, som Kong Hans mønte lod*») and later 5 Hvide.
- G = 24 ß l. (Ribe 1501). Hence 1 M l. = 1,6 → 1,67 M d., a **non-integer** ratio, by computation from these rates.

**Struck:** Hvid throughout; Skilling from c. 1497; dated Skilling and Søsling 1512.

## 9. 1534 – Oct 1537 (Christian III's war coin)

**No act** with tariff or grid. The war coin comes from Junge's accounts: 2 M and 1 M Klippinge, 4 ß, 1 ß.
Observed, not set: G = 2½ M d. (accounts 1534–36). The king's tax rate for a Gylden or Joachimsdaler was
8 M d. (1536) and 9 M d. (1539) (W s. 248); Wilcke reports it as the king's own tariff, but no act is printed.

- **1424:** Pf and Sechsling (6 Pf), common with Lübeck. No Mark or Gylden link is set.

## Reading of the chains (curator discussion 2026-11-05)

- Before 1537 the acts set two scales that meet only at a non-integer rate: the Gylden is integral in
  Skilling of the Lübeck count (24 ß: 1514, 1523), while the Danish Mark is the Hvid Mark (48 Hvide) and
  the Gylden equals it only as 2½ (Oslo 1525; accounts under Hans and 1534–36). This is the Mark
  lybsk / Mark dansk duality of `mark_lybsk_mark_dansk.md`. In 1514 the two coincide (Gylden = 72 Hvide
  = 1½ Hvid-Mark = 24 ß).
- The 1537 tariff (Reces 1540, Gotland letter 1541) sets the Gylden / Joachimsdaler at exactly 3 Mark:
  Pf → ß (12) → M (16) → Daler (3) becomes the first integer Danish chain anchored on the Daler.
  Candidate end-point of the Lybsk Møntfod by this criterion — **curator decision pending**.
- The 1 : 2 Danish : Lübeck ratio is first SET by an act in 1602; «1 : 2 from 1541» (Denar til daler
  s. 488) and «from 1582» (Wilcke 1919 s. 55) are observations.
- From 1602 the Mark PIECE leaves the account Mark (16½, 17, 18½, 20, 21, 13⅓ ß against 16 ß).
