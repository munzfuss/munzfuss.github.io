# Frederik I, Goldgulden of Schleswig/Gottorp (Galster 122) — the dating of the Berlin specimen «um 1506»

> **Subject.** One type, two die groups, three datings. The undated Rhenish-standard
> Goldgulden of Frederik I struck at Schleswig/Gottorp (Galster 122, Lange 9/9A,
> Sieg 49, MB 30, Fr 14) exists in our data as the classified class
> `unified-dk-bruun-14741` (royal_slesvig, `rhinsk_gylden_fod` I.a, year 1531)
> and as the stray `ikmk-18239610` (Berlin, `seed_unsorted`, «um 1506»). The
> question put by the curator 2026-10-14: is 1506 an error, or are these two
> coins 25 years apart? This file holds everything assembled on that question.
>
> **Status 2026-10-14:** research done to the limit of accessible sources; the
> curator's merge / year decision is pending (§9). Dannenberg's 1859 text — the
> only source of the 1506 — could not be read (§4.2).
>
> Related: `rhinsk_gylden_fod.md` (the standard), `sh_ducal_zone_husum_1514.md`
> (the duke's Husum silver), `instruments/1514_moentordning_malmoe.md`,

**The photographs.** The picture danskmoent.dk shows under Galster 122
(`gif/f1_g122.gif`, captioned «G:122, 23 mm, Au 3,25 gr») **is the Berlin
specimen** — same dies, same 3,25 g. Numista 417865 uses the same photograph
while dating the type «1531 (1523–1533)». So of the three sources that show the
«second» picture, two merely reproduce the Berlin coin; the visual group
«Berlin / Galster / Numista» is one object, not three attestations. The Bruun
coin is the only other photograph in play.

**Two die groups, one type.** Galster lists 122 as «1531, u. år» with «Schou 1–4
og 1–4» (the dated and the undated series) and then registers the Berlin coin
separately as «u. år, Schou ej» — an undated variant unknown to Schou 1926.
Bruun's undated coin is Schou 1. Galster therefore already treats the two as
distinct die groups of one type. The differences (legend spelling
HOLSACI / HOLSATI, SLESWICENSIS / SLESVICENSIS; the cross before vs behind the
saint; straight vs diagonal shield arrangement; the ring-dots) are die-group
differences, not evidence of a different type. Bruun's «Lange 9a» and IKMK's
«Lange 9 A» cannot both be the same Lange variant if Lange's letter marks the
reverse legend — one of the two citations is loose; Lange 1908 (paper) would
settle it.

## §2. Where the «1506» comes from

- IKMK: «um 1506», publications «H. Dannenberg, Goldmünzen des XV. und XV.
  [sic] Jahrhunderts, Zeitschrift für Münz-, Siegel- und Wappenkunde Neue Folge
  1859–1862, 48 Nr. 44 (dieses Stück); Chr. Lange's Sammlung
  schleswig-holsteinischer Münzen und Medaillen (1908) Nr. 9 A (abweichende
  Rs.-Umschrift)». museum-digital (SMB object 172233) gives «1506 [circa]»,
  place Schleswig, no explanation of the date.
- The article: Dannenberg, *Goldmünzen des XV. und XVI. Jahrhunderts*, ZfMSWK
  NF (1859–62) pp. 40–54, 85–115, 144–176, pls. 2–5; reprinted in *Studien zur
  Münzkunde des Mittelalters* (1984) pp. 598–676, pls. 24–27. Kluge's reprint
  list titles it «Goldmünzen des XV. und XVI. Jahrhunderts [Fund Chörau in
  Anhalt, nach 1546]». pp. 40–54 are the Chörau find list (Anhalt Nr. 43,
  Schleswig Nr. 44, Saxony Nr. 26a, Bremen Nr. 90 …).
- The find. The hoard of gold coins discovered 1850 at Chörau near Dessau
  (Stenzel 1876, *Numismatische Studien* I p. 9; SMB record of the Anhalt
  gulden 145891: «aus dem 1850 in Chörau bei Dessau entdeckten Schatzfund …
  von dem Numismatiker und Fundbearbeiter Hermann Dannenberg erworben»). Seven
  Chörau pieces are in our IKMK cache (18206240, 18318331, 18239556, 18239610,
  18239612, 18239557, 18306111), dated by IKMK 1463–1514. Kluge dates the
  hoard's closure **after 1546**. The find therefore gives the Berlin coin a
  terminus ante quem of c. 1546 and **no support whatever for 1506**.
- The text of p. 48 Nr. 44 could not be read: the Neue Folge is not on
  archive.org (only 1841–1846 are), not on Google Books in full view,
  HathiTrust and Numista sit behind Cloudflare challenges. What Dannenberg
  based the year on is unknown. **Hypothesis, unverified:** he read the ducal
  title «D HOLSACI» as pre-royal and set a round year inside Frederik's ducal
  reign; nothing in the coin itself carries a date.

## §3. What the Danish literature knows of Duke Frederik's coinage — nothing before c. 1514, no gold at all

- Galster 1934, *Reynold Junges Møntmesterregnskaber 1534–40*, p. XI (PDF
  p. 14): «Husum, hvor Hertug Frederik kort før 1514 havde oprettet en Mønt.
  Møntmesteren her var Jørgen Drewes … Vistnok 1522 havde Hertug Frederik
  indrettet Møntsmedje paa Gottorp, i en Bastion Nordøst for Slottet … og
  Udmøntningerne i Husum var rimeligvis indstillet, da Møntergaarden omved
  1529 solgtes».
- Wilcke VII (1950) pp. 184–186 (PDF 39–41 of `wilcke_7-2.txt`): the 1474
  imperial privilege to Christian I to strike gold and silver; «Hertug Frederik
  benyttede denne Tilladelse til at slaa Mønt efter Rigsmøntfod i Byen Husum»;
  mintmaster Jørgen Drewes; the Mark 1514 (18,9 g), ½ Mark, ¼ Mark, 2 Mark
  1522, Husumdaler 1522; «Jürgen Drewes blev, selv efter at Mønten var
  forflyttet til Slesvig, boende i Huset». The comparison table p. 186 (PDF
  42) «Hertug- og Kongemønter» lists for the DUKE only silver (Mark, ½, ¼,
  Dobbeltskilling, Skilling, 2 Mark 1522, Husumdaler); gold appears only in
  the KING's part — Nobel 25/2 1524, «Rhinsk Gylden … 72 · 18 K. · 750 ·
  3,249 · 2,437».
- Galster's Frederik I list (danskmoent `f1galst.htm`): Husum — 114 Sølvgylden
  (Husumdaler) 1522, 115 Mark 1514, 116 8 Skilling, 118 2 Skilling 1514
  (Oldenburg arms), 119 2 Skilling 1514–1522 (Schleswig lions), 120 Skilling
  1514/1516/u. år, 121 Søsling 1516; Gottorp — **122 Rhinsk gylden 1531, u.
  år**, 123 Sølvgylden klipping u. år, 125–129 small silver. Galster's note on
  118/119: «Mønten er slået, mens Frederik endnu var Hertug af Gottorp; dansk
  kongetitel fik han først 1523». No ducal gold anywhere.
- Frederik's gold begins as king: Nobel 1524 (Rigsraadets Brev 25 Feb 1524 /
  Kongebrev), Rhinsk gylden Malmø 1527 (Galster 59, Schou 1–3), then Gottorp
  (Galster 122, 1531 and undated), Ungersk gylden 1531 (Galster 46).
- **The standard itself is of 1524** (curator's point, 2026-10-14). The Rhenish
  gulden at 72 to the mark and 18 K — the standard the Gottorp gulden is struck
  on (Galster: .750, 3,19 g target) — enters Frederik's coinage with his
  Møntordning of 1524 (Rigsraadets Brev 25 Feb 1524 and the Kongebrev; Wilcke
  VII p. 186 table «Rhinsk Gylden … 72 · 18 K. · 750 · 3,249 · 2,437»; dossier
  `instruments/1524_moentordning_frederik_i.md`). Before that act Frederik had
  no gold ordinance of his own; a gulden on this standard earlier than 1524 is
  therefore improbable on the legal as well as on the mint-historical side.

**Consequence.** The legend «MO NO AVREA SLESWICENSIS» names the Schleswig
mint, i.e. Gottorp. That mint was set up c. 1522 (Galster 1934; Wilcke's
«forflyttet til Slesvig»). Before that the duke's only mint was Husum (from
shortly before 1514), whose coins read HVSEMENSIS. **A Schleswig-mint coin
cannot be of 1506.** The earliest year the sources allow is c. 1522 — still
ducal for a few months — and every known gold of Frederik is royal.

## §4. Tests that do NOT decide

### 4.1 Heraldry — the Norway lion

Hypothesis tested: «the lion of Norway marks a coin struck after Frederik's
election as king of Norway (1524)». **Refuted.** The Husumdaler 1522, struck
while Frederik was duke, already bears a helmeted shield quartered
**Norway / Schleswig / Holstein / Stormarn** with Oldenburg in the centre
(Künker lot 417341 description; Galster 114; Lange 12; Dav. 8235) — the very
four shields of the gulden. The shield set is the Oldenburg duke's own, not a
royal addition.

### 4.2 The ducal title

«D HOLSACI» / «D HOLSATI» stands on the Gottorp gulden dated **1531** as well
(Galster 122 «1531»; Bruun's cluster titles the coin «Friedrich I ducal
Goldgulden»). Frederik kept the ducal formula on his Gottorp coinage as king;
the title dates nothing.

### 4.3 Weight and fineness

3,25 g (Berlin) · 3,24 g (Bruun) · 3,23 / 3,15–3,22 g (Galster 1934 table for
the Slesvig Guldgylden 1534 and 1536) — all one standard (72 to the mark,
18 K). No chronological signal.

### 4.4 The find

Chörau closes after 1546 (Kluge) — consistent with 1523–1533, with 1534–1546
and with 1506 alike; it excludes nothing in the range.

## §5. The visual difference — what it can and cannot tell

The two die groups are real and already registered (Schou 1 vs «Schou ej»;
Lange 9 vs 9A). Whether they are also two EMISSIONS separated in time is
undecidable from what we hold:

- The 1531-dated dies (Schou 1531: 1–4) would tell which style group carries
  the date, and hence which group is the «dated» emission. Our Galster 122 page
  shows only the Berlin photograph; Schou 1926, Lange 1908 and Jensen/Skjoldager
  *Tronraneren* (2021, Frederik I's Gottorp gold around p. 211) are paper.
- The straight-cross arrangement of the Berlin reverse resembles the Christian
  III Gottorp gulden of 1534 (Galster 130: «Forside: våbenskjolde, bagside:
  rigsæble» — a different type, orb reverse) no more than the diagonal one
  does; no stylistic chain can be built from the two pictures alone.
- **Open hypothesis, no evidence for or against:** the Berlin variant («Schou
  ej», HOLSACI / SLESWICENSIS) could be the earliest Gottorp emission of
  c. 1522–1523, the Bruun variant (Schou 1) the royal one. Nothing in any
  source says so; it stays a question for Schou/Lange/Tronraneren.

## §6. Verdict on the dating

1. «um 1506» is an isolated dating of 1859, carried unchanged by IKMK, without
   stated grounds, and incompatible with the foundation of the Schleswig/Gottorp
   mint c. 1522, with the Møntordning of 1524 that first sets Frederik's
   Rhenish-gulden standard, and with the absence of any ducal gold in Galster
   and Wilcke.
   It is to be treated as a source error of Dannenberg's (hypothesis as to its
   origin in §2), not as a competing attestation.
2. The sources' range for the undated Gottorp gulden: **c. 1522–1533**
   formally, **1523–1533** in every Danish catalogue (Galster «1531, u. år»;
   Bruun / NumisMaster «ND (1523–1533)»).
3. The Berlin coin and the Bruun coin are one type (Galster 122, Lange base 9)
   in two die groups. Merging them is §9.4-clean; the year must not be the
   union «1506, 1531».
