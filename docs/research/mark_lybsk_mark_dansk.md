# Mark lübisch and Mark dansk, c. 1300–1514 — which mark, what ratio, why two

> **Status:** internal research dossier (role-1), opened 2026-10-16 at the curator's request («треба
> розуміти про яку марку йдеться коли не уточнюють, наприклад … 11½-Mark, і знати співвідношення … і
> коли і через що виникла диференціація»). Feeds the 11½-Markfod card (`luebischer_muenzfuss.md`).
>
> **Question this dossier settles:** (1) which mark a bare «N Mark» means in the sources for the Lübeck
> standard and for King Hans' coinage; (2) the ratio Mark lübisch : Mark dansk at each documented date;
> (3) when and why the Danish money of account parted from the Lübeck one.
>
> **Excluded, and where it lives:** the weight marks (Cologne / Stockholm / troy — `cologne_mark_weight_16c.md`);
> the Swedish mark of account (survey `hans_silver_1481_1513.md` §10.5); Frederik I's 12-ß Mark of 1524 and
> later (8-Gylden-Fod card, `8_gylden_fod`) — only touched in §4 as the next step; Norway's «norrøn mark»
> (Holst, survey §10.4).

## §2. The two marks — definitions as the sources give them

- **Mark lübisch** (Jesse «M.», Bobzin «Ml.», Wilcke «M lybsk», «Stæder M» in Danish documents) = 16
  Schilling lübisch = 192 Pfennig (M5: «*Den lybske myntmark deltes i 16 skillinger = 192 penninger*»).
- **Mark dansk** (Wilcke «M d.», «danske M», «danske Penge»): a Danish money of account of 16 ß dansk.
  Under Hans the Danish ß is reckoned at **3 Hvide**, so 1 Mark dansk = 48 Hvide (M3 s. 149:
  «*M dansk à 16 ß à 3 Hvide*»).
- Wilcke's own wording of the split (M3 s. 149): «*delt Valutaen i M lybsk à 16 ß lybske eller danske
  Skillinger og M dansk à 16 ß à 3 Hvide. Af den første gik 1½ M eller 24 ß paa Gyldenen, af den sidste
  2 M = 32 ß = 96 Hvide*» — i.e. **Hans' Danish Skilling coin counted in the Lübeck mark**; the Danish
  mark was the mark of Hvide.
- Danish documents of Hans' time name both side by side: 31 May and 2 July 1495 Hans acknowledges a
  debt of «*100 Stæder M Penge og 300 M danske M i saadan Mønt som i Danmark gængs er*» (M2 s. 142).

## §3. Which mark is meant in «11½ Mark»

- Jesse's «*War damals die feine Mark zu 11 M. 8 s ausgebracht*» (1492 Doppelschillinge; M1) is a Lübeck
  recess figure in Lübeck money: 11 Mark 8 ß **lübisch** per fine mark. Bobzin's «N-Mark-Fuß» is defined
  as «*1 Ml. = 1/N feine Mark Silber*» (M6), Ml. = Mark lübisch.
- So «11½-Markfod» = 11½ **Mark lübisch** per fine Cologne mark (Schilling 103½ from the 9-lot mark,
  1468 = 1492). It is not a Danish-mark figure.
- **Recomputed in Mark dansk** (our arithmetic, from the ratios of §4): 11½ × 1,6 = **18,4 Mark dansk**
  at the earlier ratio, 11½ × 5/3 ≈ **19,2** at the later one. The card's unit block states the marks and
  ratios; it does not state this product.

## §5. Why two marks — what the sources say

- **Long-term**: Danish pennies were debased far below the Lübeck ones from the late 13th c. (M1). A Danish
  mark of pennies and a Lübeck mark were therefore different amounts of silver long before Hans; the 1424
  treaty made the new coins common for a few years only.
- **Under Hans** (M3 s. 149, Wilcke's explanation, verbatim): «*det særlig var Kong Hans' store
  Udmøntninger af daarlige Krigshvide, der havde bragt Forvirring i hele Pengeregningen og delt Valutaen i
  M lybsk … og M dansk*». The Skilling coin was struck near the Lübeck Schilling (survey §5; card dossier
  §3.4), the Hvid well below the Lübeck Witten and valued as a Lübeck 3-penny (M4 s. 5). A Skilling
  therefore cost 4–5 Hvide instead of 3, and the mark counted in Hvide (Mark dansk) fell against the mark
  counted in Skillings (= Mark lübisch). This is Wilcke's reading; no other source in our cache states a
  cause.

## §7. Consequences for the 11½-Markfod card

- The units block already shows both marks and both ratios (`f65cba1`). The card name «11½-Markfod» is a
  Mark-lübisch figure (§3); a clarifying «(Mark lübisch)» in the card text would remove the ambiguity —
  curator's call.
- Any figure in Mark dansk on the card must state the ratio it uses (1,6 early, ≈ 1,67 later).
