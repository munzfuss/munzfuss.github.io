## §2. Model and name

- **The system.** Denmark coined on the Wendish (Lübeck) system from Margrete onwards: Witten
  (Hvid) 4 Pf., Sechsling (Søsling) 6 Pf., Schilling 12 Pf., Mark = 16 ß = 192 Pf. (L1 s. 99–104;
  L6). After the 1424 Danish–Hanseatic treaty broke, «*Nur die Anlehnung des dänischen
  Münzwesens an das der Städte blieb bestehen*» (L1 s. 103).
- **Norway** joined it with Hans: håndfæstning 1 Feb 1483, coin «*jeffngod … wid Danske
  penninge*» (L5 s. 3–4); L6: the Oldenburgs' Norwegian coins «*efter det lybske myntsystem*».
- **Holstein** (imperial fief, the Danish king its count from 1460, duke from 1474): Christian
  I bound himself and his heirs to strike «*ingen mønt … uden den som til Lübeck og Hamburg er
  gængs og gæv*» (L7).
- **Name.** «lübischer Münzfuß» is the scholars' term (Grautoff 1836, Jesse); the recesses
  write «lubesche munte / mark»; Danish texts «lybsk mynt»; Wilcke «lybsk Møntfod» (survey
  §10.3). Card strings per D14.

1512 copper clause, two readings: (a) ½ lot of copper added to a 15-lot mark → 15/16½ = 14,55 lot, 16,35 g fine per piece; (b) the mark brought down by ½ lot → 14½ lot, 16,30 g. Same result within 0,3 %, and Jesse's next step (1515: 12 to the 14½-lot mark) is at 14½ lot — the clause does not affect the card.

1424 fineness read 2026-10-15 from the page image of the danskmoent PDF (Jesse s. 102: «*mit 11¼ Lot Feingehalt mit 3 Grän erlaubtem Remedium zu 42 Wurf = 168 Stück aus der Mark (1,35 g)*», «*aus 7¼lötigem Silber zu 3½ Mark + 2 ß = 696 Stück (0,33)*»); the coingallery transcription agrees; the PDF text layer had garbled the ¼. Checks: 1424 Sechsling «1,35 g» in Jesse vs our 1,392 — Jesse's mark weight ≈ 227 g (cf. his
19,392 g for a 1492 Mark, survey §5 — open). The 1492 Schilling 1,271 g fine matches Wilcke's
«1,269–1,103» for the Lübeck ß c. 1500 (L2 7-2 s. 150–151).

**Reading (this dossier's):** the Skilling was struck to the Lübeck Schilling and debased
from it; the Hvid never reached the Lübeck Witten (0,355 g) and was valued as a Lübeck
3-pfennig («densche drelinge», L3; L4 s. 5). The card should present the Lübeck standard as
the frame and the Danish tiers as executed below it — the role of the Hvid is that of small
change (Wilcke 7-1 s. 36: «*kun Skillemønt*»).

### §3.3 Norway and Gotland under Hans

Same tiers (Hvid, Søsling, Skilling at Bergen; Hvide at Oslo, Nidaros; Hvid at Visby). Norwegian
Hvide average 0,63 g vs Danish ≈ 0,75 g (Ernst 1940 summary); Holst's weight-base caveat
(survey §10.4) — set aside by D15 (Norway included from 1483).

## §5. Cessation

The card ends for Denmark in **1513**: Christian II's håndfæstning («*thue mark giør fyldest
for en … rhinsk Gylden*») and Blicher's grid set a Danish mark of 32 ß = 96 Hvide per Gylden
detached from the Lübeck ß (Wilcke 7-2 s. 149–151: «*Forholdet til den lybske ß ophævet*»);
1514 returns to 24/72 per Gylden but with the Sølvgylden as head coin — a new standard
(`8_5_gylden_fod`). Indirect end by the curator's rule for undeclared ends (2026-10-11):
mark approximate if the card shows it as a status end.
