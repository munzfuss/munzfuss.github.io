## §2. Model and name

- **The system.** Denmark coined on the Wendish (Lübeck) system from Margrete onwards: Witten
  (Hvid) 4 Pf., Sechsling (Søsling) 6 Pf., Schilling 12 Pf., Mark = 16 ß = 192 Pf. (L1 s. 99–104;
  L6). After the 1424 Danish–Hanseatic treaty broke, «*Nur die Anlehnung des dänischen
  Münzwesens an das der Städte blieb bestehen*» (L1 s. 103).
- **Norway** joined it with Hans: håndfæstning 1 Feb 1483, coin «*jeffngod … wid Danske
  penninge*» (L5 s. 3–4); L6: the Oldenburgs' Norwegian coins «*efter det lybske myntsystem*».
- **Holstein** (imperial fief, the Danish king its count from 1460, duke from 1474): Christian
  I bound himself and his heirs to strike «*ingen mønt … uden den som til Lübeck og Hamburg er
  gængs og gæv*» (L7).
- **Name collision (found 2026-10-15).** Bobzin («Münzfüße», hagen-bobzin.de, cache `scripts/cache/bobzin/muenzfuesse.*`) uses «*Der Lübecksche oder lübische Münzfuß (oder Lübischer Kurantfuß)*» for the **1726** Hamburg–Lübeck 34-Mark-Fuß — our card `11_333_thaler` (11⅓-Thaler-Fuß, historical name «Kurantmøntfod»). A card named «Lübischer Müntzfuß» for the 15th-c. standard can be read as that 1726 standard — curator to decide whether D14 needs a qualifier (put to the curator 2026-10-15). **Provenance of the 1726 usage (checked 2026-10-15):** Bobzin's passage is a near-verbatim modernisation of **Meyers Konversations-Lexikon 1888, Bd. 11, S. 891**, lemma «Münzfuß» (de.wikisource MKL1888:Münzfuß): «*der Lübecksche oder lübische M., nach welchem die Mark zu 11⅓ Thlr. oder 34 Mk. gerechnet wurde*» … «*so daß dieser lübische M. thatsächlich ein 35-Markfuß war*» … «*Der eigentliche lübische M. galt in Lübeck und im Kleinverkehr in Hamburg*» (read via WebFetch, not yet cached). So it is not Bobzin's coinage: the 19th-c. reference literature uses «lübischer Münzfuß» for the 1726 34-Mark-Fuß. Bobzin and MKL are ONE reading, not two witnesses (§9.4b logic). Not found (web search, 2026-10-15): «Lübischer Courantfuß / Kurantfuß» outside Bobzin; Wikipedia DE «Lübische Mark» uses no «lübischer Münzfuß» at all. A mid-1830s overview (Wienbibliothek PDF 4812073, search snippet only, unread) lists a «Lübeck mark standard» used in Lübeck and Hamburg; the 1875 Oldenburg notice says «lübisch-hamburgische Kurantwährung». **Correction (same day):** the sentence first written here — «no source found applying «lübischer Münzfuß» to the 15th-c. standard» — was wrong, written without re-reading the survey: the 15th-c. sense is the scholars' term of Grautoff 1836 («über den lübischen Münzfuß bis 1463») and Jesse (survey §10.3), and Wilcke 1950 7-1 s. 143 writes «*efter lybsk Møntfod*» for Hans' 1483 Norwegian clause (also 7-6 for the 1570s). So BOTH senses are literature terms (19th–20th c.); neither is a period compound. The collision is real either way. **Curator D19 (2026-10-15):** D14 stands; the collision is set aside until the 1726 card is reworked (both are Lübeck standards, at different times).
- **Name.** «lübischer Münzfuß» is the scholars' term (Grautoff 1836, Jesse); the recesses
  write «lubesche munte / mark»; Danish texts «lybsk mynt»; Wilcke «lybsk Møntfod» (survey
  §10.3). Card strings per D14.

### §3.1a The «Mark-Fuß» measure — Mark lübisch per fine Cologne mark

Bobzin («Lübisches Münzsystem», hagen-bobzin.de; cache `scripts/cache/bobzin/muenzverein_wendisch.*`)
states the Lübeck standard as the number of Mark lübisch (16 ß) struck from one fine mark,
e.g. «*1 Ml. = 1/13 feine Mark Silber ≈ 18g Silber (13-Mark-Fuß)*» for the 1502/1506 Markstück.
Applied to the defining coins (Bobzin's own figures where he gives them, else recomputed from §3.1):

**For the card (plan step 3.2, proposal, curator's call):** the whole-standard defining value
can be given in Bobzin's measure — the Lübeck standard of the 1468/1492 Rezess = **11½ Mark
lübisch from the fine Cologne mark** (Schilling 103½ from the 9-lot mark) — with Hans' early
Skilling at that level and the debasement to ≈ 15–17 Mark by 1512. «11½-Mark-Fuß» is Bobzin's
analytic form, not a period name; the card name stays «Lybsk Møntfod» / «Lübischer Müntzfuß».
Bobzin's caveat (SOURCES §6.5): an amateur compilation — his chronology is cross-checked against
Jesse above (1502/1506 agree; Bobzin's 1461 has no Jesse counterpart in our cache).

**Reading (this dossier's):** the Skilling was struck to the Lübeck Schilling and debased
from it; the Hvid never reached the Lübeck Witten (0,355 g) and was valued as a Lübeck
3-pfennig («densche drelinge», L3; L4 s. 5). The card should present the Lübeck standard as
the frame and the Danish tiers as executed below it — the role of the Hvid is that of small
change (Wilcke 7-1 s. 36: «*kun Skillemønt*»).

### §3.3 Norway and Gotland under Hans

Same tiers (Hvid, Søsling, Skilling at Bergen; Hvide at Oslo, Nidaros; Hvid at Visby). Norwegian
Hvide average 0,63 g vs Danish ≈ 0,75 g (Ernst 1940 summary); Holst's weight-base caveat
(survey §10.4) — set aside by D15 (Norway included from 1483).

Rough-weight ratios to the early Skilling (2,47 g): Søsling 0,60, Hvid 0,36. Our own data (no fineness)
has the same top weights (Hvid 0,90 / 0,88, Skilling 2,65, Søsling 1,57).

**Reading (hypothesis, curator's call):** against the early full Skilling the Hvid carries ≈ ¼ of its
silver — the Lübeck 3-pfennig value Eriksson s. 5 reports («värdesattes endast till 3 penningar
lübsk») and Wilcke 7-2 s. 149's «Skillinger … kostede baade 4 og 5 Hvide i Stedet for de almindelige
3»; against the debased 1512 Skilling the nominal ratios (Hvid 4 Pf = ⅓ ß, Søsling 6 Pf = ½ ß) hold
in silver. That fits Eriksson's dating of the Søsling to the later reign (s. 10). Would be settled by
dated Søslinger / Hvide with assays; only two Søslinger are assayed. Rates in the sources: Wilcke 7-2
s. 149 (4–5 Hvide per Skilling); Eriksson s. 11 after Galster 1936 s. 165 (Hvid «till underkurs mot
skillingen»); Blicher 1 Aug 1513 restores 3 Hvide per ß (Wilcke 7-2 s. 149–150).

### §3.4b Did Hans follow the Lübeck 13-Mark-Fuß after 1502? (2026-10-15, curator question — open)

Mark lübisch per fine mark, recomputed from Eriksson tab. 7 (fine = weight × Ag %; 233,856 g mark):
Lübeck 1468/1492 Schilling 11½; Lübeck Markstück 1502/06 13 (per ß 1,124 g); Lübeck Schilling 1515 13¼.
Hans 9 lod 11,5 · 8 lod 11,8–14,8 · 7 lod 13,3–18,2 · 6 lod ≈ 15,7 · dated 1512 15,3–17,9 (one 1512
outlier, 3,13 g @ 41,63 %, 11,2).

**Reading (hypothesis):** the 8-lod and best 7-lod Skillinger sit at the 13-Mark level, so a middle
stage on the Lübeck 1502/06 step is possible; but the 1512 coins (15–18) are well below 13–13¼ — the
end-of-reign debasement went beyond the Lübeck step. No source says Denmark took part in the
1502/1504/1506 recesses (Wendish towns only, Jesse) or that Hans adopted them; the 9/8/7-lod
Skillinger are undated (only the 1512 ones carry a year). Would be settled by: dated 8/7-lod
Skillinger, hoards with closing dates (Jensen et al. 1992), Märcher 2018.

**Sources checked for a dated fineness series (2026-10-15):** none in our cache gives one. Eriksson 2017
(assays grouped by lod; only the 1512 pieces dated; s. 10: undated high-silver Skillinger «måste vara
präglade före 1512»; Skilling minting perhaps only after 1497 — Bangbostrand hoard, c. 1490, 210 Hans
Hvide and no Skillinger; Möllegården, Skillinger only); Wilcke 7-1 s. 144 (no ordinance → no comparison
possible; Mårten 1542 ≈ 2,7 g «lidt over 5½ Lod» = the coins melted in 1542, an end state); Wilcke 7-2
s. 150–151 (Lübeck ß c. 1500 «1,269 — 1,103 gr.» fine, i.e. 11½ → 13¼); Galster galkult (Skilling
«nogenlunde god», Hvide bad; Hans Sever mintmaster in Malmø 1492–1512); Ernst 1940 summary (Danish
Witten 245–280 ‰ vs Lübeck Witten 1502 1,08 g @ ≈ 328 ‰). Not in cache, would be needed: Galster 1972
*Unionstidens udmøntninger* (paper), Märcher 2018 *Denar til Daler* s. 426–495 (paper), the DMS hoard
corpus (Jensen et al. 1992) for closing dates of hoards with 9/8/7-lod Skillinger, Østergaard 1995,
Stefke on the Lübeck side.

### §3.5 Mark coins under Hans? (2026-10-15)

No specimen is known. The only witness is Mårten Myntskrivare's 1542 melt account (Svartsjö):
«*danska hwiter, Konung Hanses skellinger och Marckestycker*» (Wilcke 7-1 s. 139). Wilcke (s. 141)
concludes «*Det maa da have været lybske Markstykker, som Kong Hans har ladet udmønte*»; Galster 1947
(hansgals): Hans' «Thaler» / Sølvgylden known only as casts and fantasy pieces, none from a find or a
document; Eriksson 2017 s. 6: «*inte känt att det skulle finnas några marker från Hans (Galster 1972,
23f). Förmodligen kan dessa markmynt syfta på silvergyllen*». Note: had Hans struck a Mark lübsch c.
1502–1513, the Lübeck Markstück of those years was already on the 13-Mark-Fuß (§3.1a), not 11½.

### §3.6 Danish spelling «mark» / «marck» (2026-10-15, curator question)

From the period texts Wilcke prints: Swedish Arboga 1480 «*wågen mark*» (7-1 s. 118); Haandfæstning
22 July 1513 «*thue mark giør fyldest*» (7-2 s. 149, from Erik Krabbe's later copy); Blicher 1 Aug
1513 «*veien mark*», «*weien Kølns mark*» (7-2 s. 149–150, partly paraphrased by Wilcke); Rigsraad
1524 «*vegne marck*», «*vegenn marck*» (7-2); 1529 «*marck danske*», «*marck siølff*» (7-2); Mårten
1542 (Swedish) «*Marckestycker*» beside «*mark*»; coin legends «*I MARCK DANSKE 1543*» (Holst). So:
«mark» in the Hans / 1513 texts, «marck» in Danish chancery texts from the 1520s. Caveat: the 1513
texts reach us through later copies and Wilcke's partial normalisation — not settled without the
originals (Danske Breve fra Middelalderen / Diplomatarium Danicum online).

## §5. Cessation

The card ends for Denmark in **1513**: Christian II's håndfæstning («*thue mark giør fyldest
for en … rhinsk Gylden*») and Blicher's grid set a Danish mark of 32 ß = 96 Hvide per Gylden
detached from the Lübeck ß (Wilcke 7-2 s. 149–151: «*Forholdet til den lybske ß ophævet*»);
1514 returns to 24/72 per Gylden but with the Sølvgylden as head coin — a new standard
(`8_5_gylden_fod`). Indirect end by the curator's rule for undeclared ends (2026-10-11):
mark approximate if the card shows it as a status end.
