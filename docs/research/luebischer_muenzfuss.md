## §2. Model and name

- **The system.** Denmark coined on the Wendish (Lübeck) system from Margrete onwards: Witten
  (Hvid) 4 Pf., Sechsling (Søsling) 6 Pf., Schilling 12 Pf., Mark = 16 ß = 192 Pf. (L1 s. 99–104;
  L6). After the 1424 Danish–Hanseatic treaty broke, «*Nur die Anlehnung des dänischen
  Münzwesens an das der Städte blieb bestehen*» (L1 s. 103).
- **Norway** joined it with Hans: håndfæstning 1 Feb 1483, coin «*jeffngod … wid Danske
  penninge*» (L5 s. 3–4); L6: the Oldenburgs' Norwegian coins «*efter det lybske myntsystem*».
- **Holstein** (imperial fief, the Danish king its count from 1460, duke from 1474): Christian
  I bound himself and his heirs to strike «*ingen mønt … uden den som til Lübeck og Hamburg er
  gængs og gæv*» (L7).
- **Name collision (found 2026-10-15).** Bobzin («Münzfüße», hagen-bobzin.de, cache `scripts/cache/bobzin/muenzfuesse.*`) uses «*Der Lübecksche oder lübische Münzfuß (oder Lübischer Kurantfuß)*» for the **1726** Hamburg–Lübeck 34-Mark-Fuß — our card `11_333_thaler` (11⅓-Thaler-Fuß, historical name «Kurantmøntfod»). A card named «Lübischer Müntzfuß» for the 15th-c. standard can be read as that 1726 standard — curator to decide whether D14 needs a qualifier (put to the curator 2026-10-15). **Provenance of the 1726 usage (checked 2026-10-15):** Bobzin's passage is a near-verbatim modernisation of **Meyers Konversations-Lexikon 1888, Bd. 11, S. 891**, lemma «Münzfuß» (de.wikisource MKL1888:Münzfuß): «*der Lübecksche oder lübische M., nach welchem die Mark zu 11⅓ Thlr. oder 34 Mk. gerechnet wurde*» … «*so daß dieser lübische M. thatsächlich ein 35-Markfuß war*» … «*Der eigentliche lübische M. galt in Lübeck und im Kleinverkehr in Hamburg*» (read via WebFetch, not yet cached). So it is not Bobzin's coinage: the 19th-c. reference literature uses «lübischer Münzfuß» for the 1726 34-Mark-Fuß. Bobzin and MKL are ONE reading, not two witnesses (§9.4b logic). Not found (web search, 2026-10-15): «Lübischer Courantfuß / Kurantfuß» outside Bobzin; Wikipedia DE «Lübische Mark» uses no «lübischer Münzfuß» at all. A mid-1830s overview (Wienbibliothek PDF 4812073, search snippet only, unread) lists a «Lübeck mark standard» used in Lübeck and Hamburg; the 1875 Oldenburg notice says «lübisch-hamburgische Kurantwährung». **Correction (same day):** the sentence first written here — «no source found applying «lübischer Münzfuß» to the 15th-c. standard» — was wrong, written without re-reading the survey: the 15th-c. sense is the scholars' term of Grautoff 1836 («über den lübischen Münzfuß bis 1463») and Jesse (survey §10.3), and Wilcke 1950 7-1 s. 143 writes «*efter lybsk Møntfod*» for Hans' 1483 Norwegian clause (also 7-6 for the 1570s). So BOTH senses are literature terms (19th–20th c.); neither is a period compound. The collision is real either way. **Curator D19 (2026-10-15):** D14 stands; the collision is set aside until the 1726 card is reworked (both are Lübeck standards, at different times).
- **Name.** «lübischer Münzfuß» is the scholars' term (Grautoff 1836, Jesse); the recesses
  write «lubesche munte / mark»; Danish texts «lybsk mynt»; Wilcke «lybsk Møntfod» (survey
  §10.3). Card strings per D14.

### §3.1b How the sources state the size of the standard (2026-10-16, curator question)

- **Jesse** (coingallery reproduction, counted 2026-10-16): the piece-count form «N Stück aus der x-lötigen
  Mark» 43 times — the recesses' own form (e.g. 1468 «*103½ Stück aus der 9lötigen Mark*»); the value of
  the fine mark («die feine Mark zu N Mark») 9 times, used to compare standards across time. For this
  standard verbatim: «*Verglichen mit dem Münzfuß der Doppelschillinge von 1492 … War damals die feine Mark
  zu 11 M. 8 s ausgebracht, wurde sie 1522-30 zu 14 M. 6 s bis 15 M. 10 s ausgemünzt*» — 11 M 8 ß = 11½
  Mark per fine mark, from the 1492 Doppelschilling (69 / 12-lot mark → 92 per fine mark = 184 ß). So
  «11½» is Jesse's figure, not only our recomputation (§3.1a).
- **Bobzin** («Lübisches Münzsystem»): value-of-the-fine-mark and «N-Mark-Fuß» throughout, but from 1502
  on (13-, 13 7/10-, 13¼-, 12⅘-Mark-Fuß); for 1461 «1 feine Mark Silber = 10 Ml. und 6 bis 12 ßl.»; the
  1468 / 1492 standard itself is not stated by him.
- **Wilcke 7-2 s. 150–151**: fine weight per ß («1,269 — 1,103 gr.»); **Eriksson**: lod + weight per piece.
- **Formula on the card**: «103½ Schilling / rough Cologne mark / 9 Lot» is the recess form — the 9-lot
  mark is the alloyed (rough) mark of 9-lot silver; 103½ pieces per FINE mark would be wrong (184).

**One standard, two measures (2026-10-16, curator question):** 103½ ß per ROUGH mark of 9-lot silver =
103½ × 16/9 = 184 ß = 11½ Mark lübisch per FINE mark; the 1492 Doppelschilling (69 per 12-lot mark) gives
the same 92 × 2 = 184 ß. The weight mark is the Cologne one — Jesse (M1, «Metrologie»): «*Für das
Silbergeld aber sind kölnische und lübische Mark identisch*», the Cologne mark being «*im deutschen Norden
das herrschende Münzgewicht*». The ratio does not depend on the mark's gram weight, which Jesse discusses
as unsettled for the Middle Ages (Hilliger 210,24 g, Guilhermoz 229,4558 g, traditional 233,855 g); his own
1468 figure (1,278 g fine per ß) implies ≈ 235 g, Galster takes ≈ 230 g for Denmark c. 1500 (§3.1, §9).

Reading: the period instruments state pieces per rough mark at a lot fineness; «11½ Mark» is the
historians' comparative measure (Jesse 1492, Bobzin's «N-Mark-Fuß» for later steps). Both are in the
literature; the card name uses the second, the card formula the first.

### §3.1c The Lübeck denominations of 1468 / 1492 and their Danish counterparts (2026-10-16)

**The recesses (Jesse, M1 = L1, coingallery reproduction):**
- 15 June 1468, Hamburg–Lübeck: «*der den Schilling weiter auf 103½ Stück aus der 9lötigen Mark
  herabsetzte (1,278 g fein) und außerdem und offenbar zum gleichen Münzfuß für 1/20 der Gesamtsumme
  Sechslinge sowie Doppelschillinge zu 69 aus der 12lötigen Mark und endlich Blafferte und Pfennige
  vorsah. Die Hälfte der Gesamtsumme sollte aber in Schillingen als der wichtigsten Münzsorte ausgeprägt
  werden.*» Soll weights of the three silver sorts: «*3,39, 2,27 und 1,13 g*». New Doppelschillinge show
  the city's saint (Hamburg Gaedechens 745–49 ≈ 3,28 g; Lübeck Behrens 65, 3,35 g average).
- 1492 recess, 31 articles — the aim (1483 proposal of the Hamburg burgomaster Hermann Langenbeck) is the
  «*ewigen werigen und beständigen Lubeschen mark*», to order the silver money «*na den Lubeschen gulden*»
  and not follow the sinking Rhenish gulden: «*Deshalb wurde der Münzfuß von 1468 beibehalten, also 69
  Doppelschillinge aus der 12 und 103½ Schillinge aus der 9lötigen Mark geprägt. Die Sechslinge wurden
  etwas schlechter, zu 188-190 aus der 8lötigen Mark, ausgebracht. Für die Blafferte und Pfennige endlich
  war ein Münzfuß von 4½ Mark = 864 Pfennigen oder 432 Blafferten aus 6lötigem Silber festgesetzt (0,27
  und 0,54 g, fein 0,1 und 0,2 g).*» Quota: the two coarse sorts in equal parts, 1100 Mark (Lübeck 400,
  Hamburg 300, the other two 200 each).
- Later (context only): the 1540s talks name «*als Scheidemünzen Doppelschillinge und Sechslinge*» (61–63
  from the 7½-lot mark) — by then the Doppelschilling had become small change; in 1468/1492 it was a
  coarse sort at the full Schilling standard.

### §3.1d How the Danish denominations sat in the Lübeck standard — legal link or market? (2026-10-16, analysis, no card change)

**What the sources give:**
1. **No Danish act of Hans fixes any Danish coin to the Lübeck foot.** No Danish Møntordning of Hans
   survives (Galster: «*Der er ikke bevaret nogen dansk Møntordning fra Kong Hans*»); Wilcke 7-1 s. 128:
   «*I Danmark synes netop ingen Møntordning at være udstedt*», s. 144: «*foreligger … ikke nogen
   Møntordning*». Galster also reads the 1513 grid as repeating «ældre, tabte danske Møntordninger» —
   so a lost Hans ordinance cannot be excluded; its content is unknown.
2. **The 1483 Haandfæstning ties Norway to Denmark, not Denmark to Lübeck.** The act (Gullbekk s. 3–4):
   «*alle disse tre steder jevngod mynt med*» Danish pennies. «*o: efter lybsk Møntfod*» is Wilcke's gloss
   («o:» = «that is»), 7-1 s. 143, not the act's words. ⚠ The card row «Haandfæstning 1483» quotes the
   gloss as if it were the act — to fix (curator).
3. **The only hard Danish–Lübeck link in our sources is the 1424 common coinage** (Jesse): new Sechsling /
   Hohlpfennig to circulate equally in the three realms and the four towns; broken within years.
4. **The Skilling coin was struck to the Lübeck Schilling and reckoned as Lübeck money** — de facto, not
   by any surviving decree: weights «*motsvarade mycket bra de lübska*» (Eriksson s. 5); the early 9-lod
   Skilling ≈ 1,27 g fine = 103½ / 9-lot (§3.4); Wilcke 7-2 s. 149: «*Kong Hans' Skillinger … regnedes god
   for lybsk Valuta, 20 eller 24 paa Gyldenen*». So «103½ Schilling» describes Hans' Skilling only in the
   sense that the coin matched it (early stage); no source says «103½ Skilling» of Denmark.
5. **The Danish money of account was the mark of Hvide** (16 ß à 3 Hvide; Wilcke 7-2 s. 149), and its
   relation to the Skilling coin and to the Gylden FLOATED: Skilling coin 4 Hvide (1497), elsewhere 5;
   Gylden 2 M d (toll 1503) → 2½ M d (1503 «senere», 1508) (Wilcke 7-1 s. 134, 142). These are values from
   accounts, tolls and debts — circulation values, not decreed rates.
6. **The first Danish legal fixing of the units is 1513**: Haandfæstning 22 July (2 Mark dansk = 1 Gylden)
   and Blicher's letter 1 Aug (16 ß per M, 3 Hvide per ß, with fineness and pieces per mark) — and Wilcke
   reads it as cutting the Lübeck tie («*Forholdet til den lybske ß ophævet*», 7-2 s. 151).

**Reading (this dossier's, curator's call):** under Hans the standard was Lübeck's; the Danish side had
(a) a Skilling coin that tracked the Lübeck Schilling physically and was counted in Lübeck money, and (b)
a Danish money of account in Hvide whose value against the Skilling and the Gylden was set by circulation
(accounts, tolls, contracts) — no fixed legal rate is documented. «103½ Skilling» is not attested; the
Danish units relate to the foot through the Skilling coin's physical match and through market rates.

**The Lübeck foot expressed in Danish units (our arithmetic, to show the spread, not a source figure):**
184 ß per fine mark (11½ Mark lübisch) — with the Skilling coin at 4 Hvide = 736 Hvide = 15⅓ Mark dansk;
at 5 Hvide = 920 Hvide = 19⅙ Mark dansk; via Wilcke's 20 ß l. = 2 M d. = 18,4 Mark dansk; via 24 ß l. =
2½ M d. ≈ 19,2. ⚠ Internal tension in Wilcke: 20 ß lybsk per Gylden with 96 Hvide gives 4,8 Hvide per ß
lybsk, not 4; his own pairing (7-2 s. 149) is 4 Hvide ↔ 96 Hvide per Gylden at 24 ß per Gylden. Not
resolvable from our sources.

### §3.4c Did the Danish Skilling follow Lübeck's step to 13 Mark (1502/06)? (2026-10-16, curator question — open, leaning «consistent»)

**For** (none decisive):
1. **The 8-lod Skillinger sit on the 13-Mark level.** Eriksson tab. 7, fine = weight × Ag % (C): 8 lod Malmø
   1,236 / 0,987 g (11,8 / 14,8 Mark per fine mark), 8 lod København 1,183 / 1,100 g (12,4 / 13,3); the
   13-Mark Schilling is 1,124 g, the 13¼ one 1,103 g. The 8-lod group brackets the 13-Mark value; the 9-lod
   group sits on 11½ (1,273 / 1,266 g). 7 lod already lower (0,80–1,10 g, 13,3–18,2).
2. **Lübeck's own Schilling was sinking in the same years**: «*1,269 — 1,103 gr.*» c. 1500 (Wilcke 7-2
   s. 150–151) = 11½ → 13¼ (C); Hans' Skillinger «*regnedes god for lybsk Valuta*» (s. 149) — counted as
   Lübeck money whatever Lübeck's ß was worth.

**Against / not established:**
3. **No act, letter or account says Denmark took the 1502–1506 step.** The recesses are the Wendish towns'
   (Jesse); Denmark is not named as a party after 1424.
4. **The reckoning does not mark it:** the Gylden at 24 ß lübisch is Lübeck's value both in 1461 and in 1502
   (Bobzin: «*1 Rheinischer Gulden … = 1½ Ml. = 24 ßl.*» in both places), so «24 Skilling per Gylden» in
   Denmark does not date a move to 13.
5. **The 8-lod Skillinger are undated**; whether they belong after 1502 is exactly the open point. Holding a
   level near 13 could also be drift from 11½ that happens to pass through 13.
6. **Stage III goes far beyond 13** (1512: 15–18), so if Denmark followed the step it did not stay on it.
7. Wilcke's «*lybske Markstykker, som Kong Hans har ladet udmønte*» (7-1 s. 141) would, if true, be coins on
   the 13-Mark Markstück standard — but no specimen exists and Galster / Eriksson read the 1542 «Marckestycker»
   otherwise (§3.5).

**Verdict:** the data are CONSISTENT with Denmark's Skilling following Lübeck to about 13 Mark per fine
mark in stage II (and then falling further), but this cannot be stated as fact. Would settle it: hoards with
closing dates that contain 8-lod Skillinger (DMS, Jensen et al. 1992), Galster 1972, Märcher 2018; any
Danish account naming the Skilling's weight / fineness between 1500 and 1511.

### §3.4d Curator's fixed position on the 13-Mark question and the research it leaves open (2026-10-16)

**Fixed (curator, on the evidence of §3.4c):** at some point within the Lybsk Møntfod the Danish Skilling
did stand at the level of the 13-Mark standard (the 8-lod group, ≈ 1,0–1,24 g fine). Whether it got there
by FOLLOWING Lübeck's 1502/06 step or simply by debasement passing through that level is not known.

**Indirect support for an orientation on the Lübeck system until 1513:** Wilcke reads the 1513 grid as
cutting the tie — «*Forholdet til den lybske ß ophævet … paa Bekostning af Skillingen og dens Forbindelse
med den lybske M og ß*» (7-2 s. 151) — which implies the tie existed before 1513. Together with «*regnedes
god for lybsk Valuta*» (s. 149) this makes the Lübeck system a plausible factor in the Danish level, not
proof that Denmark took the 1502/06 step.

**Lübeck after the step — did it debase further?** (Bobzin, «Lübisches Münzsystem»; Jesse)

## §5. Cessation

The card ends for Denmark in **1513**: Christian II's håndfæstning («*thue mark giør fyldest
for en … rhinsk Gylden*») and Blicher's grid set a Danish mark of 32 ß = 96 Hvide per Gylden
detached from the Lübeck ß (Wilcke 7-2 s. 149–151: «*Forholdet til den lybske ß ophævet*»);
1514 returns to 24/72 per Gylden but with the Sølvgylden as head coin — a new standard
(`8_5_gylden_fod`). Indirect end by the curator's rule for undeclared ends (2026-10-11):
mark approximate if the card shows it as a status end.

### §6d KMM 331973 «Hans · Lund · penning · Hbg. 1» — not a coin of King Hans (2026-10-16; declassified)

**The record.** `scripts/cache/kmk/331973.json` (Inv. FP 6086.1): authority «Hans», place «Lund»,
nominal «penning», typeNumber «Hbg. 1», creation 1483–1513, find «NV for Stangegård, Åker s.»
(Bornholm), no weight, no description, no image (natmus page checked 2026-10-16). No other FP 6086.x
object is in the KMM cache. In our data: seed `kmk-331973` → `unified-kmk-331973` (`danish_realm`),
`luebischer_muenzfuss` I `kurant` by the bulk Hans decision of 2026-10-15
(`classification_decisions/danish_realm.yml`, reason «King Hans / Rigsrådet 1481 / …»); `why` shows no
coin-specific decision. Seed carries `catalog.hauberg: '1'`, `mint: Lund`, `mint_verified: true`.

**Which Hauberg.** Four Hauberg catalogues come into question; none numbers a coin of Hans:
- *Danmarks Myntvæsen og Mynter i Tidsrummet 1241–1377* (1885) — numbering restarts **per ruler and
  per mint**; «Lund 1» exists under every king from Erik Plovpenning to Valdemar Atterdag
  (danskmoent konkord.htm, `scripts/cache/danskmoent/konkordans/konkord.htm`: «1241-1250 Erik
  Plovpenning Lund: Hauberg = MB», «1319-1332 Christoffer 2. Lund: Hbg/MB: 1/524, …»).
- *Danmarks Myntvæsen i Tidsrummet 1377–1481* (Aarbøger for nordisk Oldkyndighed og Historie 1886,
  s. 135 ff.; archive.org `y1886a87aarbger00norduoft`) — ends 1481 by title; it has **no running
  catalogue numbers**, only figures: Fig. 1 (s. 153) is the Flensborg Firepenning («Saavel Omskriften
  som Neldebladet vise, at denne Mynt, den ældste kjendte fra Flensborg …»); Erik af Pommern's Lund
  sterlings are Fig. 7–9 (s. 162).
- *Myntforhold og Udmyntninger i Danmark indtil 1146* (1900) and *Danmarks Myntvæsen i Tidsrummet
  1146–1241* (1906) — before the period by title.
- *Gullands Myntvæsen* (1891) — Gotland; the danskmoent Hans list cites it as Hauberg 102–108 (Visby).

**The KMM precedent in the same field.** Sibling record `kmk-353178`: authority «Christian 2», place
Roskilde, nominal penning, typeNumber «Hbg. 4, MB 558». The konkordans gives «1319-1332 Christoffer 2.
… Roskilde: Hbg/MB: … 4/556-561» — MB 558 falls inside Hauberg 1885 Christoffer II Roskilde no. 4. So
KMM uses Hauberg 1885 with per-mint numbers, and in that record the ruler name is not the one the
catalogue number belongs to (Christoffer II, not Christian II). A third sibling, `kmk-306180`
(Roskilde, «Hbg. 1»), carries authority «?».

**No Lund mint under Hans.** (i) Hauberg 1886, s. 136: «Kronen omsider nødtes til at standse
Udmyntningen ved de forskjellige Myntsteder, sidst i Lund, hvor Mynten nedlagdes ved Aaret 1377»
— the end of the 13th–14th-c. penning coinage. (ii) Jensen, J. S.: *Regionale forskelle på udmøntning
og møntbrug i Danmark ca. 1050 – ca. 1500*, Hikuin 35 (2008), s. 158: «Møntstedet i Lund blev lukket
lidt ind i 1440’erne, og i stedet blev produktionen flyttet til Malmø» (after Erik af Pommern's
sterlings and hollow pennies, c. 1420–35); s. 159: «under kong Hans oprettet nye møntsteder, både i
København og i Ålborg». (iii) danskmoent hansgals.htm (after Galster): Hans' only penning is the Oslo
hulpenning (Galster 156); no Lund. (iv) danskmoent 1penning.htm: the last Lund penning listed is Erik
af Pommern's «ca. 1400-1420, Lund. Hulpenning; krone i strålekrans (Galster 5 note)».
No archiepiscopal Lund coinage under Hans was found (web search 2026-10-16; hypothesis (b) unsupported,
not disproved — MEC 10 / Karlsson & Steen Jensen on late-medieval Lund would settle it).

**Conclusion (computed from the above, not a source statement).** «Hbg. 1» + «Lund» is a Hauberg 1885
(1241–1377) type number — Lund no. 1 of one of eight rulers, which one the record does not say — or
less likely Hauberg 1900/1906. The authority «Hans» and the 1483–1513 dating are therefore very
probably a KMM data-entry error of the «Christian 2» kind; hypothesis: «Hans» stands for a Johannes
(e.g. an archbishop) or is a slip — unverified. Without weight, description or image the ruler cannot
be recovered from the record. It is not an 11½-Markfod coin.
