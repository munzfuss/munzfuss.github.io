**Rules that follow (for the card):**
- In stage I the Skilling = the Lübeck Schilling — ratios through the Skilling are usable there.
- Hvid and Søsling are small change; their rates against the Skilling were set in circulation (documented
  for the Hvid). Do not convert them by a fixed rate; show the attested values per stage.
- Mark dansk is a mark of Hvide; Mark lübisch : Mark dansk = (Hvide per ß lübisch) ÷ 3 (**C**) — I: 1,6

- Søsling: nominally ½ Skilling; by silver a small-change coin; dated only 1512 → stage III (II at the
  earliest if the undated Kbh / Bergen pieces are older — not known).

**Skilling — begins c. 1495–1497.**

  Skilling (Eriksson s. 9).
- Gribshunden purse (sank June 1495): Hvide only; Ingvardson 2022: «*the sample size … is too small to draw
  definitive conclusions*» — weak terminus post quem.
- 1497, first written mention: «*4 Hvide ß, som Kong Hans mønte lod*» (Wilcke 7-1 s. 142) — terminus ante quem.
- Eriksson s. 9 allows a start «*först efter 1497*»; the month of the 1497 mention is not known.
- Silver: undated Skillinger carry the highest fineness (Eriksson: only 6 of 22 undated below 40 %); the six dated

**Skilling 7 lod vs 6 lod — can 7 be c. 1510–1512 and 6 be 1512–1513? (curator question 2026-10-21)**
- Eriksson tab. 7 (the only assays; our own data carry weights only, no fineness for Hans' Skillinger):
  dated 1512, Malmø G29: **3 pieces at 7 lod** (37,72–41,63 % Ag, 2,22–3,13 g) **and 3 at 6 lod** (33,28–35,90 %,
  2,45–2,66 g). Undated («1481-1511» in Eriksson's table): 9 lod 3, 8 lod 5, 7 lod 8, 6 lod 6.
- So 6 lod is already struck in 1512 — it cannot be confined to a later 1512–1513 stage by the dates. Whether 7 lod
  precedes 6 lod within 1512 the year alone cannot show. No Skilling dated 1513 is known (Galster: «*1512, u.år*»);
  Hans died 20 Feb 1513.
- The lod groups are Eriksson's rounding of % Ag (6 lod = 37,5 %, 7 lod = 43,75 %): the 1512 «7 lod» pieces start at
  37,72 %, a hair above 6 lod; the 1512 sample is in fact one continuous spread 33–42 %.
- **Reading (H):** falling fineness 9 → 8 → 7 → 6 lod over time; 7 lod c. 1510–1512, 6 lod 1512 (– Feb 1513). Not
  separable into two periods on present data. Would be settled by more dated 1512 assays, or by die links between
  undated 6-lod pieces and the dated 1512 dies.
- **Curator's reading (2026-10-21), adopted as the working dating:** fineness was lowered over time, so a higher lod
  most probably precedes a lower one. 1512 is the boundary year of two periods: the period ending in 1512 has
  **7 lod**, the one beginning in 1512 has **6 lod**; both fall within 1512, which is why the dated 1512 pieces show
  both. → P4 (c. 1510 – 1512) = 7 lod; P5 (1512 – Feb 1513) = 6 lod. Still a reading of the assays (no 7/6 order
  is attested inside 1512); it fixes the rule for dating undated pieces (TODO ED).

**Re-examination of the P2/P3 border «1500/03 – 1503/06» (curator question 2026-10-21: does 1503–1506 deserve its
own period?).** The fractional border mixes three different events:
- **c. 1500/01 — Danish rate change 2 → 2½ M d per Gulden.** 2½ in 1500 (DCH), 1501 (Mulich), 1502, 1503–1508;
  Piorr: «*zumindest zwischen 1500 und 1505 … üblicherweise 2,5 Mark*» (hans_rates §3). Since the Gulden stayed at
  24 ß l., this is a fall of the HVID (3 → 2,4 Lübeck Pf), not of the Skilling: the struck Skilling passed for
  1 ß l. both before (4 Hvide, 1497) and after (5 Hvide).
- **1503 — the forced toll rate** (Kolding / Ribe tolls at the old 2 M d, accounted at 2½, 1503–1507/10; Enemark via
  Piorr). An administrative counter-measure to the 1500/01 change, not a coin event.
- **Aug 1504 decision / 29 Dec 1506 Rezess — Lübeck's 13-Mark step** (Lübeck ß 1,271 → 1,124 g).
No Danish coin event dated 1503–1506 is attested. **So 1503–1506 as such does not stand as a period.**

What the silver says (our arithmetic, H): the 9-lod Skilling (1,24–1,27 g) equals the Lübeck ß of 1468 (1,271), the
8-lod Skilling (København 1,10–1,18; whole group 0,99–1,24) the Lübeck ß after the 13-Mark step (1,124). If the
Danish Skilling kept pace with the Lübeck Schilling (it passed for 1 ß l. throughout), the 9 → 8 lod step belongs
at Lübeck's step (1504/06), not at the Danish rate change. The Hvid steps differently: 6 lod (≈ 0,27–0,30 g, ≈ 3 Pf
l.) before 1500/01, 5 lod (≈ 0,18–0,24 g, 2,4 Pf l.) after. **The two denominations change at different times.**

**Proposal for the curator (H):** replace «1495/97 – 1500/03 / 1503/06 – 1510» by
- P2 **1495/97 – 1500**: Skilling 9 lod = 4 Hvide; Hvid 6 lod; Gulden 2 M d.
- P3 **1501 – 1506**: Skilling 9 lod = 5 Hvide (= 1 ß l. 1,271 g); Hvid 5 lod; Gulden 2½ M d; tolls at a forced 2 M d
  from 1503.
- P4 **1506 – 1510**: Skilling 8 lod (≈ Lübeck ß 1,124 g); Hvid 5 lod; Gulden 2½ M d.
- then 1510 – 1512 (Skilling 7 lod) and 1512 – Feb 1513 (Skilling 6 lod), as decided.
The 1506 border is Lübeck's Rezess, applied to the Danish Skilling by the parity argument above — a reading, not
an attested Danish act. Would be settled by a dated assay of an 8-lod Skilling, or by a Danish text tying a
Skilling change to the Lübeck step.

**Where «1500/03» and «1503/06» came from, and does 1501–1506 contradict anything? (curator, 2026-10-21)**
- *«c. 1500/03» (end of 9-lod Skilling, start of stage II)* — from §0.1 (written 2026-10-15/16, before the DCH full
  pass): «*Gylden = 2½ M d ved Aarhundredskiftet*» (Wilcke 7-1 s. 134) plus Wilcke's 1503 mixed rates (toll 2 M d,
  account 2½) read as «transition c. 1503». The later reads moved the attested change earlier: DCH «*2½ mr. fore
  en rinsk guldene*» already in **1500**, Mulich 1501, 1502; Piorr: 2½ «*zumindest zwischen 1500 und 1505*». The
  1503 «2 M d» is the forced toll rate (Enemark via Piorr, 1503–1507/10), not the market. So «1503» was a stale,
  low-resolution estimate; the attested lower bound is 1500 («bis etwa 1500» for 2 M d, 1500 for 2½).
- *«c. 1503/06» (start of 8-lod Skilling)* — hans_rates §6: two readings kept side by side — (a) the 8-lod
  Skilling = the Lübeck ß after the 13-Mark step (1506); (b) «*they could also be c. 1500–1505 debasement passing
  the level*», with 1503 (start of the forced toll rate, read as a sign of a worse coin) as its anchor. «1503/06»
  is the two readings written as one range.
- *Does «9 lod = 5 Hvide, c. 1500 – 1506» contradict any source?* Checked against the register: no.
  «*5 Hvide for 1 ß*» (Wilcke 7-1 s. 142, undated) fits; Lütke 1503–06 (124 Hvide / G, Gulden 24 ß l.) is a Hvid
  rate and says nothing about the Skilling; the forced toll rate 1503 does not require a Skilling debasement (the
  Hvid fall of 1500/01 is enough to explain it); Enemark's «*Abwertung der dänischen Schillingmünzen*» ends the
  forced rate after 1510 — compatible with 8 lod from 1506 and with 7 lod by 1510. Reading (b) is NOT refuted
  either: no 8-lod Skilling is dated, so a debasement already in 1500–1505 stays possible. The choice of 1506 rests
  on the parity argument, not on a date.
- *Correction to the proposal:* the 2½ rate is attested in 1500, so the boundary year is **1500**, not 1501
  (cf. the 1512 decision: a boundary year belongs to both periods) → **1495/97 – 1500** and **1500 – 1506**.
- Still fractional: the P1/P2 border «1495/97» (Gribshunden June 1495 weak, 1497 first mention). Not resolved.

**Consequence for G149 (Bergen Søsling, 0,54 g fine).** ½ ß l. = 0,635 g before 1506, 0,562 g after: 0,54 g is −4 %
from the post-1506 value and −15 % from the pre-1506 one → **1506 – 1510** under the proposal (not «1503 – 1510»).
Søsling evidence checked for a contradiction (2026-10-21): no Danish or Norwegian Søsling of Hans is dated before

from one specimen with a fineness of unstated basis. The Bergen weights (1,38–1,684 g) run above the Danish
undated Søslinger (1,31–1,47 g), consistent with an earlier, better issue (H).

### §0.6 Periodisation 1481 – Feb 1513 — ACCEPTED by the curator 2026-10-21 («1 – так, 2 – так»)

## §2. Model and name

- **The system.** Denmark coined on the Wendish (Lübeck) system from Margrete onwards: Witten
  (Hvid) 4 Pf., Sechsling (Søsling) 6 Pf., Schilling 12 Pf., Mark = 16 ß = 192 Pf. (L1 s. 99–104;
  L6). After the 1424 Danish–Hanseatic treaty broke, «*Nur die Anlehnung des dänischen
  Münzwesens an das der Städte blieb bestehen*» (L1 s. 103).
- **Norway** joined it with Hans: håndfæstning 1 Feb 1483, coin «*jeffngod … wid Danske
  penninge*» (L5 s. 3–4); L6: the Oldenburgs' Norwegian coins «*efter det lybske myntsystem*».
- **Holstein** (imperial fief, the Danish king its count from 1460, duke from 1474): Christian
  I bound himself and his heirs to strike «*ingen mønt … uden den som til Lübeck og Hamburg er
  gængs og gæv*» (L7).
- **Name collision (found 2026-10-15).** Bobzin («Münzfüße», hagen-bobzin.de, cache `scripts/cache/bobzin/muenzfuesse.*`) uses «*Der Lübecksche oder lübische Münzfuß (oder Lübischer Kurantfuß)*» for the **1726** Hamburg–Lübeck 34-Mark-Fuß — our card `11_333_thaler` (11⅓-Thaler-Fuß, historical name «Kurantmøntfod»). A card named «Lübischer Müntzfuß» for the 15th-c. standard can be read as that 1726 standard — curator to decide whether D14 needs a qualifier (put to the curator 2026-10-15). **Provenance of the 1726 usage (checked 2026-10-15):** Bobzin's passage is a near-verbatim modernisation of **Meyers Konversations-Lexikon 1888, Bd. 11, S. 891**, lemma «Münzfuß» (de.wikisource MKL1888:Münzfuß): «*der Lübecksche oder lübische M., nach welchem die Mark zu 11⅓ Thlr. oder 34 Mk. gerechnet wurde*» … «*so daß dieser lübische M. thatsächlich ein 35-Markfuß war*» … «*Der eigentliche lübische M. galt in Lübeck und im Kleinverkehr in Hamburg*» (read via WebFetch, not yet cached). So it is not Bobzin's coinage: the 19th-c. reference literature uses «lübischer Münzfuß» for the 1726 34-Mark-Fuß. Bobzin and MKL are ONE reading, not two witnesses (§9.4b logic). Not found (web search, 2026-10-15): «Lübischer Courantfuß / Kurantfuß» outside Bobzin; Wikipedia DE «Lübische Mark» uses no «lübischer Münzfuß» at all. A mid-1830s overview (Wienbibliothek PDF 4812073, search snippet only, unread) lists a «Lübeck mark standard» used in Lübeck and Hamburg; the 1875 Oldenburg notice says «lübisch-hamburgische Kurantwährung». **Correction (same day):** the sentence first written here — «no source found applying «lübischer Münzfuß» to the 15th-c. standard» — was wrong, written without re-reading the survey: the 15th-c. sense is the scholars' term of Grautoff 1836 («über den lübischen Münzfuß bis 1463») and Jesse (survey §10.3), and Wilcke 1950 7-1 s. 143 writes «*efter lybsk Møntfod*» for Hans' 1483 Norwegian clause (also 7-6 for the 1570s). So BOTH senses are literature terms (19th–20th c.); neither is a period compound. The collision is real either way. **Curator D19 (2026-10-15):** D14 stands; the collision is set aside until the 1726 card is reworked (both are Lübeck standards, at different times).
- **Name.** «lübischer Münzfuß» is the scholars' term (Grautoff 1836, Jesse); the recesses
  write «lubesche munte / mark»; Danish texts «lybsk mynt»; Wilcke «lybsk Møntfod» (survey
  §10.3). Card strings per D14.

### §3.1b How the sources state the size of the standard (2026-10-16, curator question)

- **Jesse** (coingallery reproduction, counted 2026-10-16): the piece-count form «N Stück aus der x-lötigen
  Mark» 43 times — the recesses' own form (e.g. 1468 «*103½ Stück aus der 9lötigen Mark*»); the value of
  the fine mark («die feine Mark zu N Mark») 9 times, used to compare standards across time. For this
  standard verbatim: «*Verglichen mit dem Münzfuß der Doppelschillinge von 1492 … War damals die feine Mark
  zu 11 M. 8 s ausgebracht, wurde sie 1522-30 zu 14 M. 6 s bis 15 M. 10 s ausgemünzt*» — 11 M 8 ß = 11½
  Mark per fine mark, from the 1492 Doppelschilling (69 / 12-lot mark → 92 per fine mark = 184 ß). So
  «11½» is Jesse's figure, not only our recomputation (§3.1a).
- **Bobzin** («Lübisches Münzsystem»): value-of-the-fine-mark and «N-Mark-Fuß» throughout, but from 1502
  on (13-, 13 7/10-, 13¼-, 12⅘-Mark-Fuß); for 1461 «1 feine Mark Silber = 10 Ml. und 6 bis 12 ßl.»; the
  1468 / 1492 standard itself is not stated by him.
- **Wilcke 7-2 s. 150–151**: fine weight per ß («1,269 — 1,103 gr.»); **Eriksson**: lod + weight per piece.
- **Formula on the card**: «103½ Schilling / rough Cologne mark / 9 Lot» is the recess form — the 9-lot
  mark is the alloyed (rough) mark of 9-lot silver; 103½ pieces per FINE mark would be wrong (184).

**One standard, two measures (2026-10-16, curator question):** 103½ ß per ROUGH mark of 9-lot silver =
103½ × 16/9 = 184 ß = 11½ Mark lübisch per FINE mark; the 1492 Doppelschilling (69 per 12-lot mark) gives
the same 92 × 2 = 184 ß. The weight mark is the Cologne one — Jesse (M1, «Metrologie»): «*Für das
Silbergeld aber sind kölnische und lübische Mark identisch*», the Cologne mark being «*im deutschen Norden
das herrschende Münzgewicht*». The ratio does not depend on the mark's gram weight, which Jesse discusses
as unsettled for the Middle Ages (Hilliger 210,24 g, Guilhermoz 229,4558 g, traditional 233,855 g); his own
1468 figure (1,278 g fine per ß) implies ≈ 235 g, Galster takes ≈ 230 g for Denmark c. 1500 (§3.1, §9).

Reading: the period instruments state pieces per rough mark at a lot fineness; «11½ Mark» is the
historians' comparative measure (Jesse 1492, Bobzin's «N-Mark-Fuß» for later steps). Both are in the
literature; the card name uses the second, the card formula the first.

### §3.1d How the Danish denominations sat in the Lübeck standard — legal link or market? (2026-10-16, analysis, no card change)

**What the sources give:**
1. **No Danish act of Hans fixes any Danish coin to the Lübeck foot.** No Danish Møntordning of Hans
   survives (Galster: «*Der er ikke bevaret nogen dansk Møntordning fra Kong Hans*»); Wilcke 7-1 s. 128:
   «*I Danmark synes netop ingen Møntordning at være udstedt*», s. 144: «*foreligger … ikke nogen
   Møntordning*». Galster also reads the 1513 grid as repeating «ældre, tabte danske Møntordninger» —
   so a lost Hans ordinance cannot be excluded; its content is unknown.
2. **The 1483 Haandfæstning ties Norway to Denmark, not Denmark to Lübeck.** The act (Gullbekk s. 3–4):
   «*alle disse tre steder jevngod mynt med*» Danish pennies. «*o: efter lybsk Møntfod*» is Wilcke's gloss
   («o:» = «that is»), 7-1 s. 143, not the act's words. ⚠ The card row «Haandfæstning 1483» quotes the
   gloss as if it were the act — to fix (curator).
3. **The only hard Danish–Lübeck link in our sources is the 1424 common coinage** (Jesse): new Sechsling /
   Hohlpfennig to circulate equally in the three realms and the four towns; broken within years.
4. **The Skilling coin was struck to the Lübeck Schilling and reckoned as Lübeck money** — de facto, not
   by any surviving decree: weights «*motsvarade mycket bra de lübska*» (Eriksson s. 5); the early 9-lod
   Skilling ≈ 1,27 g fine = 103½ / 9-lot (§3.4); Wilcke 7-2 s. 149: «*Kong Hans' Skillinger … regnedes god
   for lybsk Valuta, 20 eller 24 paa Gyldenen*». So «103½ Schilling» describes Hans' Skilling only in the
   sense that the coin matched it (early stage); no source says «103½ Skilling» of Denmark.
5. **The Danish money of account was the mark of Hvide** (16 ß à 3 Hvide; Wilcke 7-2 s. 149), and its
   relation to the Skilling coin and to the Gylden FLOATED: Skilling coin 4 Hvide (1497), elsewhere 5;
   Gylden 2 M d (toll 1503) → 2½ M d (1503 «senere», 1508) (Wilcke 7-1 s. 134, 142). These are values from
   accounts, tolls and debts — circulation values, not decreed rates.
6. **The first Danish legal fixing of the units is 1513**: Haandfæstning 22 July (2 Mark dansk = 1 Gylden)
   and Blicher's letter 1 Aug (16 ß per M, 3 Hvide per ß, with fineness and pieces per mark) — and Wilcke
   reads it as cutting the Lübeck tie («*Forholdet til den lybske ß ophævet*», 7-2 s. 151).

**Reading (this dossier's, curator's call):** under Hans the standard was Lübeck's; the Danish side had
(a) a Skilling coin that tracked the Lübeck Schilling physically and was counted in Lübeck money, and (b)
a Danish money of account in Hvide whose value against the Skilling and the Gylden was set by circulation
(accounts, tolls, contracts) — no fixed legal rate is documented. «103½ Skilling» is not attested; the
Danish units relate to the foot through the Skilling coin's physical match and through market rates.

**The Lübeck foot expressed in Danish units (our arithmetic, to show the spread, not a source figure):**
184 ß per fine mark (11½ Mark lübisch) — with the Skilling coin at 4 Hvide = 736 Hvide = 15⅓ Mark dansk;
at 5 Hvide = 920 Hvide = 19⅙ Mark dansk; via Wilcke's 20 ß l. = 2 M d. = 18,4 Mark dansk; via 24 ß l. =
2½ M d. ≈ 19,2. ⚠ Internal tension in Wilcke: 20 ß lybsk per Gylden with 96 Hvide gives 4,8 Hvide per ß
lybsk, not 4; his own pairing (7-2 s. 149) is 4 Hvide ↔ 96 Hvide per Gylden at 24 ß per Gylden. Not
resolvable from our sources.

### §3.4c Did the Danish Skilling follow Lübeck's step to 13 Mark (1502/06)? (2026-10-16, curator question — open, leaning «consistent»)

**For** (none decisive):
1. **The 8-lod Skillinger sit on the 13-Mark level.** Eriksson tab. 7, fine = weight × Ag % (C): 8 lod Malmø
   1,236 / 0,987 g (11,8 / 14,8 Mark per fine mark), 8 lod København 1,183 / 1,100 g (12,4 / 13,3); the
   13-Mark Schilling is 1,124 g, the 13¼ one 1,103 g. The 8-lod group brackets the 13-Mark value; the 9-lod
   group sits on 11½ (1,273 / 1,266 g). 7 lod already lower (0,80–1,10 g, 13,3–18,2).
2. **Lübeck's own Schilling was sinking in the same years**: «*1,269 — 1,103 gr.*» c. 1500 (Wilcke 7-2
   s. 150–151) = 11½ → 13¼ (C); Hans' Skillinger «*regnedes god for lybsk Valuta*» (s. 149) — counted as
   Lübeck money whatever Lübeck's ß was worth.

**Against / not established:**
3. **No act, letter or account says Denmark took the 1502–1506 step.** The recesses are the Wendish towns'
   (Jesse); Denmark is not named as a party after 1424.
4. **The reckoning does not mark it:** the Gylden at 24 ß lübisch is Lübeck's value both in 1461 and in 1502
   (Bobzin: «*1 Rheinischer Gulden … = 1½ Ml. = 24 ßl.*» in both places), so «24 Skilling per Gylden» in
   Denmark does not date a move to 13.
5. **The 8-lod Skillinger are undated**; whether they belong after 1502 is exactly the open point. Holding a
   level near 13 could also be drift from 11½ that happens to pass through 13.
6. **Stage III goes far beyond 13** (1512: 15–18), so if Denmark followed the step it did not stay on it.
7. Wilcke's «*lybske Markstykker, som Kong Hans har ladet udmønte*» (7-1 s. 141) would, if true, be coins on
   the 13-Mark Markstück standard — but no specimen exists and Galster / Eriksson read the 1542 «Marckestycker»
   otherwise (§3.5).

**Verdict:** the data are CONSISTENT with Denmark's Skilling following Lübeck to about 13 Mark per fine
mark in stage II (and then falling further), but this cannot be stated as fact. Would settle it: hoards with
closing dates that contain 8-lod Skillinger (DMS, Jensen et al. 1992), Galster 1972, Märcher 2018; any
Danish account naming the Skilling's weight / fineness between 1500 and 1511.

the classic Korshvid in four main types — Christopher (1440-48), Rigsrådet 1448 (Jensen keeps the old date;

1490'erne, dog også i København og Aalborg*»: all four were struck at Malmø, and under Hans, from the
1490s, also at København and Aalborg. The «fra 1490'erne» belongs to the København/Aalborg clause, not to
Malmø: the sentence covers Christopher's and Christian I's Korshvide, which cannot be «from the 1490s»
(corrected 2026-10-20; an earlier reading here took it as «Malmø from the 1490s»). It agrees with Galster's
Hans types — Hvid at København (G34) and Aalborg (G36) exist only under Hans — and with *Denar til daler*'s

is the later of the two datings, and his sentence carries no note number. The Korshvide's fineness «*lå mellem 200 og 250 promille*»
(same page, «Disse mønter» = these Korshvide); and a
METHOD warning — the 1440s Korshvide still occur in large numbers in hoards deposited after 1526 or even in
the 1540s, i.e. very long circulation, so a hoard's closing date dates the coin only loosely. Consequence:
even DMS would bound the 8-lod Skillinger only by a terminus ante quem, not date their issue.

### §3.4f On what terms was Denmark tied to the Lübeck standard — and was it bound to follow Lübeck's changes? (2026-10-16, curator question)

**Short answer (from the sources read):** for the KINGDOM under Christian I and Hans no instrument is known
that binds Denmark to Lübeck's coinage, let alone to Lübeck's later changes. The one real treaty (1424) fixed
specific coins and lapsed within years; afterwards there is only orientation («Anlehnung»), a Holstein-only
obligation (1460), and promises to the Danish estates whose wording for the kingdom we have not seen. The first
verbatim Danish-realm clause naming Lübeck is Frederik I's of 1523 — ten years after the «departure».

**«Since when, by which act, under which ruler» (handover from the Galster session, 2026-10-20).** Put as one line:
the standard dates from the Hamburg–Lübeck treaty of 15 June 1468 (Schilling 103½ per 9-lot mark; recesses
1483, 1492), which binds the towns only (Jesse). ~~Danish silver on that level starts with the Hvide of 1481 under
the Rigsråd … 1481 therefore marks the first coins, not an instrument.~~ **Corrected the same day (curator doubted

  svare til de tydske Pfennige, Blafferte, Dreilinge, Wittenpfennige, Sechslinge og Schillinge*»; Jesse (L1 s. 99–104)

**What «gængs og gæv / lige god ved lybsk» means as an obligation.** The formula is relational, not a
parameter: coin must PASS at par with («be as good as») the current Lübeck coin. Read literally it is dynamic —
if Lübeck debased (1502/06 step to 13 Mark), «as good as Lübeck» would be satisfied at the lower level. No source

**§3.4g-1 Did Lübeck strike on the 13-Mark standard before 1506, ahead of Hamburg? Did the towns wait for
consensus?** (2026-10-17, curator question; Jesse, coingallery reproduction)

How the union worked — Jesse, verbatim:
- Recesses are often drafts, subject to each town's decision: «*Nicht alle Münzrezesse … sind als der
  Niederschlag endgültiger und abschließender Beschlüsse anzusehen. Sie stellen vielmehr oft neue
  Verhandlungen oder die entscheidende Stellungnahme der einzelnen Städte in Aussicht, wie … 1492 und 1501*»;
  the envoys' reservation «*das "torugge bringen"*» (= referring back to their council) «*hat auch die
  Verhandlungen und Entscheidungen des Münzvereins oft arg verzögert*»; the 1492 recess was «*in einer
  besonderen Urkunde von Hamburg und Lübeck ausdrücklich bestätigt und angenommen*».
- Execution was each town's own: «*Die Ausführung im einzelnen freilich blieb den Städten selbst überlassen,
  da ja innerhalb des Münzvereins keine gemeinsame Münzstätte bestand*».
- Not all four towns always took part, and towns struck non-union sorts on their own; the rules were loosely
  kept: «*Wie wenig bindend die Vorschriften der Rezesse oft gehandhabt sind, zeigen besonders die Gepräge des
  16. Jahrhunderts, wo z. B. die Anbringung bestimmter Jahrzahlen auf den Markgeprägen durchaus nicht
  gleichmäßig gehandhabt worden ist und die Prägung der Doppelschillinge nach 1522 in den Städten zu ganz
  verschiedenen Zeiten eingesetzt hat*».

Reading (our conclusion from Jesse; no source states it in these words):
1. **For the 13-Mark Markstück there is no sign that Lübeck began earlier.** No Lübeck Mark coin dated
   1504 or 1505 is named; Lübeck's earliest Markstücke are 1506, the same year as the other three towns, and
   the type is the common one (three arms). Between Aug 1504 and 1506 the decision was paper in Lübeck too
   («*auch im folgenden Jahre noch nicht*» is said of the towns generally).
2. **But the union did not require consensus before a town struck.** In 1502 Lübeck and Lüneburg struck the
   large pieces while Hamburg and Wismar had not yet decided; in 1505 Hamburg struck on the old 1501 standard
   while the 1504 decision was pending. A single town could strike on its own — on an agreed (1501) standard,
   not ahead of an unagreed one.
3. **The 13-Mark standard became actual only with the joint 1506 outcome.** The 1504 decision fixed it
   («*Der Münzfuß war also der gleiche wie 1504 vorgesehen geblieben*» = the 1506 recess kept the 1504
   value unchanged); execution followed the 1506 agreement.
4. **Caveat on «1506» as a striking date.** The recess is of 29 Dec 1506, yet coins are dated 1506: either
   striking began earlier in 1506 (Jesse leaves the Mittfasten hope open) or the date was a fixed type date —
   Hamburg kept «1506» on all its Markstücke to mid-century, and dates on Mark coins were «*durchaus nicht
   gleichmäßig*». So «dated 1506» ≠ «struck in 1506». Whether Lübeck struck Markstücke between Mittfasten
   and December 1506 is open. Would be settled by: Lübeck mint accounts / Lübeck UB for 1506; *Hanserecesse*
   III for 1506.
5. The «1504/06» pair also stands in Jesse's list of drafts whose Münzfuß «*in der endgültigen Fassung des
   nächsten Jahres bereits wieder verändert*» was — against his own «*der gleiche wie 1504*» for the Mark.
   Unresolved inside Jesse (possibly other sorts, or the Dec 1504 charter vs the Aug 1504 decision);
   hypothesis only.

**Consequence for the Danish question (§3.4b–d):** Lübeck's large-silver step dates 1504 (decision) / 1506
(execution), not 1502. The Lübeck small change was already near 13¾ in the 1502 Witten. A Danish Skilling at
the 13-Mark level (8-lod group, §3.4c) therefore matches the Lübeck Markstück of 1506 and the Lübeck Schilling
of 1515 — still undated on the Danish side; nothing changes in the curator's fixed position (§3.4d).

*A. The Holstein clause of 1460 — its actual text and addressee.* It is art. <9> of Christian I's letter dated
Kiel, Friday before Palm Sunday (4 April) 1460 (nr. 27, «*Kristian 1. udvider og stadfæster de slesvigske og
holstenske stænders privilegier*», s. 124–126, text after the sealed original A, Landesarchiv Schleswig Urk.
Abt. 394), the supplement to the Ribe privilege of 5 March 1460:
«*Ok scholen wy vnde vnse nakomelinge in dessen landen nene munte setten sunder sodann alse to Lubeke vnd^
Hanzborch genge vnde geue is*» (s. 126) — «we and our successors shall set no coin in these lands other than
such as is current and accepted in Lübeck and Hamburg».
- **Addressee:** the bishops, prelates, knights, squires «*vnd^ den gemenen inwaneren vnses hertogdomes to
  Sleswigk vnde vnser lande Holsten vnd^ Stormaren*» — the estates of the duchy and the counties. NOT Hamburg,
  NOT the Danish Rigsråd. Christian I issues it as «*hertoge to Sleswick, greue to Holsten*».
- **Binds** Christian I «*vnde vnse nakomelinge*» — in the lands (Schleswig, Holstein, Stormarn), not the kingdom.
- **Corrections:** our §3.4f row «1460 … ↔ Hamburg» and Sømod's «*aftale … med Hamburg*» are not the act's
  wording; Hauberg s. 189 (after Hvitfeldt II 889) gives it without an addressee: «*Kong Christiern maatte ved
  Overtagelsen af Slesvig og Holsten Aar 1460 blandt Andet forpligte sig til at »Hånd eller hans Effterkommere
  skulle ingen Mynt sette, vden den som til Lybke oc Hamborg er genge oc giffue«*». Hauberg's own reading
  (s. 189): «*han kan muligvis have benyttet den indgaaede Forpligtelse til at forskaffe ikke blot
  Hertugdømmerne, men ogsaa hele Riget den større hamborgske og lybske Mynt*» — his hypothesis, not a fact;
  1473 Christian I asked Hamburg's council to coin silver for him «*vpp yuwer Münte*» (Hauberg s. 189 n. 3,
  after Londorp).
- **Afterlife:** Galster 1934 (Junge): in the duchies «*skulde den lybske Møntfod opretholdes i Henhold til
  Privilegierne af 1460*» (Christian III's time); Wilcke 7-4 s. 283 «*Hertugerne havde lovet Hansestæderne …*»
  (Wilcke names the Hanse towns as addressee, the act names the estates). Whether Hans as duke (from 1482)
  re-confirmed art. 9 — not established (ducal acts are outside *Rigslovgivning*).

*B. The Scanian Môtbok — a royal clause tying the KING's coin to the four towns.* Christian I's redaction
[1460–1474] (nr. 57, s. 217–221; dated by the royal title «*hartoghe to Sleswyck, greue to Holsten*»; text after
Langebek's Diplomatarium copy, marginal note «*Imellem 1460 og 1474*»; extract also NNÅ 1961 s. 42 nr. 800,
§78–81). Issued «*na anwysynghe vnde rade des gemenen rykes to Denemargken*» (s. 217) — on the advice of the
council of the realm of Denmark — for the merchants visiting Scania (Skanør, Falsterbo):
- art. <78>–<79> (s. 218): a tariff of coin in Lübeck money — «*en swar nobel iii. lubesche margk*», «*lub. gulden
  xxvi. schil. lub.*», «*de rynse gulden xx.ß lub*», postulat gulden 18 ß, Bishop's gulden and «schilde» 15 ß lub.,
  Arnoldus gulden 12 ß lub. (full list s. 218: heavy noble 3 M lub.; «*de lichte nobel iii. margk*» — mark not
  qualified; Lübeck gulden 26 ß lub.; Rhenish 20 ß lub.; postulat 18 ß; «*reyoldesche*» gulden 1 M; Bishop's
  gulden 15 ß lub.; «*schilde*» 15 ß lub.; Arnoldus 12 ß lub.; false new Rhenish gulden with an orb and
  «*Didericus de Brenthorst*» «*nicht wert xiiii. ß*», passing them punishable by death). Searched online
  2026-10-20 for Friis Johansen, NNÅ 1961 (which prints §78–81): not found online. Schäfer 1887 found (MDZ
  bsb00006579) and cached, `scripts/cache/hanse/schaefer_1887/`.
  — **this is Hauberg's «Christiern I's Forordning om fremmed Mynts Værdi», «1460–74,
  Langebek Dipl.» (s. 137, 189): same date, same copy, same content** (the gap in (4) is closed);
- art. <81> (s. 219): «*Item schal vnse koninglyke munte gaen like der veerleye munte*» — «our royal coin shall
  pass equal to the coin of the four» (i.e. the four towns of the Wendish union; Jesse on Margrete's Môtbok § 67:
  «*d. h. der 4 Städte des Münzvereins*»). Variant Aa: «*mynes heren*» for «*vnse koninglyke*».
- **Hans renewed it:** his redaction dated Falsterbo, 11 September 1484 (nr. 59, s. 224; lost Trenthorst MS,
  Lübeck archive copy), issued again «*Na anwisinge vnde rade des gemenen rykes to Dennemarcken*», takes over
  «*77-82*» of Christian I's text ~~— i.e. both the Lübeck tariff and art. 81~~. **Corrected 2026-10-20 from
  Schäfer 1887 (Beil. I §§ 64–67, S. 94, text after MS I = this Trenthorst MS; page image checked,
  `scripts/cache/hanse/schaefer_1887/page_0270.jpg`):** Hans' text lists the eight gold coins («*eyn swar nobel
  gelden / de lichte nobel, / de Lubessche gulden, / de Rynsche gulden, …*») **without any values**, and keeps
  § 67 «*Item schal unse koninglyke munthe hir gan gelyk der veerleye munte*». The *Rigslovgivning* apparatus
  agrees («*xx.ß lub.] mgl. Bal.*», Bal = the Wehrmann copy of the same MS). Schäfer S. XCV: «*Die Mote Johanns
  nennt acht zulässige Goldmünzen; ihr Werth pflegte durch eine Vereinbarung des dänischen Vogts und des Zöllners
  mit den Städten festgesetzt zu werden*». So the values (Rhenish 20 ß lub. etc.) are only in Christian I's
  redaction. Frederik I's redaction [1524–33] (nr. 60) does not include §77–86.
- Schäfer on the clause (S. XCVI): «*Wenn Johann ferner bestimmt, dass seine königliche Münze gehen solle gleich
  der „veerleye munthe", so kann unter dieser wohl kaum etwas Anderes verstanden werden als das Geld der vier
  Städte des wendischen Münzvereins: Lübeck, Hamburg, Wismar, Lüneburg*»; and (S. XCV–XCVI) «*seit dem
  dänisch-wendischen Münzvertrage von 1424 fand eine Annäherung statt, die bis gegen Ende des Jahrhunderts den
  Unterschied der Münzen ziemlich ausglich*» … Danish coinage «*sich ja nach dem Muster der lübischen umbildeten
  und dann dauernd enge an diese anschlossen*». Schäfer S. 80: Hans tried to change the Môte one-sidedly; the
  Hanseatic Vögte refused to attend the reading (1488, H. R. III, 2, 257; 279 § 6).
- ⚠ Jesse attributes the clause to «*das aus Margaretes Zeit stammende Môtbok*» (§ 67). In *Rigslovgivning* the
  clause is found only in Christian I's redaction nr. 57 (full-text search; Margrete's nr. 54 and Erik's nr. 55
  lack it — nr. 55 art. <58> has instead «*dat nene suluer munte anders schal gaen dyn syne eigene munte*»,
  s. 200), and Schäfer's edition (*Das Buch des Lübeckischen Vogts auf Schonen* 95–99), which Jesse would have
  used, prints the Christian I redaction (after Bal). **Hypothesis:** Jesse's «Margrete» § 67 is this
  Christian I text. Would be settled by Schäfer's edition. **Settled 2026-10-20:** Schäfer's Margrete MSS (M, M1
  Kampen) have 54 §§ only; his §§ 55 ff., § 67 included, are printed after MS I = Hans' redaction of 1484 (S. 81,
  94). Jesse's «Margarete» § 67 is Hans' 1484 text.

**What this does to the hypothesis in (2).** The Môtbok fits Wilcke's two wordings better than the Holstein act:
it is a royal (not ducal) undertaking that the king's coin shall go «like» the coin of the four towns; it was
made by Christian I AND renewed by Hans («*Hans saa vel som hans Fader*»); it was issued on the advice of the
Danish realm council («*Rigsraaderne*», 7-2 s. 147) for the Hanseatic merchants and kept in the Lübeck archive
(«*lovet Lybækkerne*», 7-1 s. 142). **Revised hypothesis:** Wilcke's promise = Môtbok art. 81 (Christian I
[1460–74], Hans 1484); the Holstein clause of 1460 is a separate, parallel obligation for the duchies. Caveats:
(a) Wilcke cites neither; (b) the Môtbok governs the Scanian fair, so its reach in the kingdom at large is not
stated in it; (c) «*gaen like*» is relational (pass at par), like «*genge vnde geue*» — no parameters. Would be
settled by Wilcke's own source, or by a contemporary text invoking art. 81.

**(3) 1468 and the recesses of 1483 and 1492 — why «confirm» at all** (Jesse, same file, «Münzfuß-Einigung von
1468» and the following paragraphs):
- 1467 Lübeck Rezess (15 Oct) chose a 101 / 9¾-lot Schilling; the four-town Münzordnung published 22 Feb 1468 left
  out the coin-standard articles, because Lüneburg and Wismar refused to cut the Gulden to 21 ß. So Hamburg and
  Lübeck alone agreed on **15 June 1468**: Schilling 103½ / 9-lot mark, Doppelschilling 69 / 12-lot, 22 ß per
  Gulden. «*Es ist nicht ganz klar ersichtlich, ob auch dieser Vertrag nur Entwurf geblieben ist. Sicher ist,
  daß die Ausmünzung alsbald begonnen hat. Der hier festgelegte Münzfuß der Schillinge findet sich fortan auch
  mehrfach (so 1483 und 1492) in den Rezessen neu bekräftigt.*»
- The Gulden rate was the moving part: 23 ß from July 1468 (Lüneburg and Wismar then joined), 24 ß from 1470,
  the Rhenish Gulden itself debased 1477 and 1490.
- 1483: Langenbeck's plan to anchor the silver on the Lübeck gold Gulden at 2 Mark = 32 ß «*nach dem Fuße von
  1468*»; the Wendish Städtetag (Lübeck, May 1483) took it up.
- 1492: the 31-article Rezess of all four towns: silver to be ordered «*na den Lubeschen gulden*», «*um nicht durch
  die Anpassung an den "heimlich" schlechter werdenden rheinischen Gulden mit dem Münzfuß der Schillinge immer
  weiter heruntergehen zu müssen und endlich auf den Stand der sundischen Mark zu kommen. Deshalb wurde der
  Münzfuß von 1468 beibehalten*»; quotas per town; first common type (Doppelschilling with the other towns' arms).
So the reconfirmations were (a) needed because 1468 was a two-town agreement of uncertain formal status and the
union's recesses were renewed rather than permanent, and (b) a deliberate choice NOT to debase the Schilling
against the falling Rhenish Gulden. The Danish card can say the 1468 standard was kept unchanged until 1506.

**(4) Did Denmark take over 1468 «silently»?** No Danish act is known (§3.4f; *Rigslovgivning* 1448–1481 has no coin
clause; ~~Christian I's valuation of foreign coin … not in *Rigslovgivning* — not read~~ **found 2026-10-20:** it
is Christian I's Môtbok redaction [1460–74], art. 79, with art. 81 tying the royal coin to the four towns', see (2a)
— the nearest thing to a Danish instrument, renewed by Hans in 1484). What is attested: Christian I's complaint of 1474 that Lübeck's new Schillinge
harmed his «*Zinsen, Renten und Zölle, die auf 23 ß = 1 fl. gegründet seien*» (Jesse). 23 ß per Gulden is exactly
the Hanseatic settlement of July 1468. So the Danish crown's revenues were fixed in Lübeck ß at the 1468 rate —
the strongest evidence that the 1468 reckoning was adopted in Denmark, without a known act. The card may state
this as the attested fact; «automatically» stays our reading.

### §3.5 Mark coins under Hans? (2026-10-15)

No specimen is known. The only witness is Mårten Myntskrivare's 1542 melt account (Svartsjö):

### §3.7 The Hvid 1440–1513 as a separate series — two systems, compatible in reckoning (2026-10-21, curator question, agreed)

**Curator's thesis (2026-10-21):** the Hvid was struck before the 103½-Schilling standard of 1468
existed, outlived it and the 13-Mark step of 1506, and changed little throughout; the Skilling (and the
Søsling as its half) was struck to the Lübeck standard and «pulled towards» it with each of its steps,
while the Hvid went on at its own, slowly drifting parameters — so Denmark had TWO systems: a Lübeck
line (Skilling, Søsling) and a Danish line (Hvid, the mark of Hvide), compatible at the level of
reckoning, with the Hvid acting as the small change (Scheidemünze) of the Lübeck frame in Denmark.
This dossier's reading agrees, with the three qualifications below. It is a reading («можна вважати»),
not a statement of any source.

**What the sources attest**

**Three qualifications**

1. *«Little changed» holds for the weight only.* The gross weight stayed at 0,72–0,90 g; the fineness
   moved within ≈ 25–34 % (best under Christoffer, falling through Christian I, raised again early
   under Hans, then 5 lod). «Unchanged» means: one denomination, one weight norm, no reform — not
   constant parameters.
2. *The unit is Wendish, the series is Danish.* Hvid = Witten = 4 Pf and its place in the reckoning
   (16 ß à 12 Pf) come from the Wendish system (§2). What is Danish is the physical coin's own
   trajectory. «Compatible in reckoning» is therefore exact: the compatibility was built in from the
   start; the divergence is in the metal.
3. *A separate series of parameters, not a separate ordinance.* No act with Hvid parameters survives
   before Blicher's letter of 1 Aug 1513 (Wilcke 7-1 s. 144); the parameters are known from assays
   only. The Skilling, too, did not merely «lag»: in 1512 it stood ⅓ below the Lübeck Schilling
   (Eriksson s. 10) — but it answered Lübeck's steps and the Hvid did not. The asymmetry of
   reaction, not the size of the lag, separates the two lines.

**Consequence for the card (curator to confirm when the Hvid rows are sorted):** the Hvid from
Christoffer to 1513 (Galster Christoffer; Rigsråd G 22; Christian I G 23a–d; Hans G 31 / 34 / 36 and the
Norwegian / Gotland Hvide) is one line and should be presented as the small-change tier of the Danish
system under the Lübeck frame, with 1468 and 1506 as background dates only — not as a Lübeck
denomination (the fraction slot «Witten» of §3.1c stays empty). Plan step 8.3 (pre-1481 silver) is
affected. Row added to `instruments/FUSS_REVIEW_FINDINGS.md` A1.

## §5. Cessation

… Roskilde: Hbg/MB: … 4/556-561» — MB 558 falls inside Hauberg 1885 Christoffer II Roskilde no. 4. So
KMM uses Hauberg 1885 with per-mint numbers, and in that record the ruler name is not the one the
catalogue number belongs to (Christoffer II, not Christian II). A third sibling, `kmk-306180`
(Roskilde, «Hbg. 1»), carries authority «?».

**No Lund mint under Hans.** (i) Hauberg 1886, s. 136: «Kronen omsider nødtes til at standse
Udmyntningen ved de forskjellige Myntsteder, sidst i Lund, hvor Mynten nedlagdes ved Aaret 1377»
— the end of the 13th–14th-c. penning coinage. (ii) Jensen, J. S.: *Regionale forskelle på udmøntning
og møntbrug i Danmark ca. 1050 – ca. 1500*, Hikuin 35 (2008), s. 158: «Møntstedet i Lund blev lukket
lidt ind i 1440’erne, og i stedet blev produktionen flyttet til Malmø» (after Erik af Pommern's
sterlings and hollow pennies, c. 1420–35); s. 159: «under kong Hans oprettet nye møntsteder, både i
København og i Ålborg». (iii) danskmoent hansgals.htm (after Galster): Hans' only penning is the Oslo
hulpenning (Galster 156); no Lund. (iv) danskmoent 1penning.htm: the last Lund penning listed is Erik
af Pommern's «ca. 1400-1420, Lund. Hulpenning; krone i strålekrans (Galster 5 note)».
No archiepiscopal Lund coinage under Hans was found (web search 2026-10-16; hypothesis (b) unsupported,
not disproved — MEC 10 / Karlsson & Steen Jensen on late-medieval Lund would settle it).

**Conclusion (computed from the above, not a source statement).** «Hbg. 1» + «Lund» is a Hauberg 1885
(1241–1377) type number — Lund no. 1 of one of eight rulers, which one the record does not say — or
less likely Hauberg 1900/1906. The authority «Hans» and the 1483–1513 dating are therefore very
probably a KMM data-entry error of the «Christian 2» kind; hypothesis: «Hans» stands for a Johannes
(e.g. an archbishop) or is a slip — unverified. Without weight, description or image the ruler cannot
be recovered from the record. It is not an 11½-Markfod coin.
