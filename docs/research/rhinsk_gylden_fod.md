## 0. Scope and exclusions

**In scope.** The gold gulden of the Rhenish tradition: the florin-descended
gold coin of the four Rhenish electors (Mainz, Trier, Köln, Pfalz), its
imperial codifications, and every Danish-Norwegian and Schleswig-Holstein
coinage struck to or measured against it — royal (Hans → Christian IV) and
ducal (Gottorp, Sonderburg lines), 1496-1664. Plus the circulation of foreign
Rhenish gulden in Denmark before any Danish coinage, because it is what made
the Danish coin necessary.

**Out of scope, with the boundary stated:**

| Excluded | Why | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Ungersk Gylden / Dukat (.986, 67/Mk) | Hungarian-Venetian ducat tradition, different fineness | `reichsdukatenfuss` + `docs/research/de_reichsdukatenfuss.md`, `ducat_origins.md` |
| Lübeck Gulden / Florin (23⅓-23½ Kt) | Lübeck deliberately stayed on the florin/ducat fineness and did NOT follow the Rhenish debasement (§2.4) | reichsdukatenfuss lineage |
| Sølvgylden / Guldengroschen / Thaler | the silver equivalent of the gold gulden — related in value, separate standard | `8_daler_fod`, `9_thaler`, `8_daler_lybsk_fod` |
| Postulatsgulden, Horn gulden | debased imitations, counted as half-gulden (§2.3) | not in project scope |
| Guldkrone (Kroneguld) | separate gold alloy and standard | `f2_guldkrone_fod`, `guldkrone` |
| Esslingen gulden of 1524 (89/Mk, 22 Kt) | an imperial variant of THIS standard, never struck in Denmark — documented in §3.2, not a phase | — |

**The discriminator against the ducat is FINENESS, never weight or the name
«Goldgulden».** Weights overlap (Rhenish ~3,25 g vs ducat ~3,49 g, worn
pieces cross). Numista's generic «Goldgulden» carries no fineness and has
misrouted two Danish coins before (N#355730 Hans, Galster 59 Frederik I).
Routing recipe, still valid:

1. Take the coin's Galster / Hede number.
2. Open its danskmoent page — the title says «Rhinsk gylden» or «Ungersk gylden».
3. Fineness if published: ~.75-.77 → Rhinsk; .986 → Ungersk.
4. Rhinsk → `rhinsk_gylden_fod`; Ungersk/Dukat → `reichsdukatenfuss`.

## 2. German track I — origin to 1490 (the standard before Denmark struck it)

### 2.1 Descent from the florin

Florence's fiorino d'oro of 1252 is the model (danskmoent 1rhingyl:
«Forbilledet for den rhinske gylden er Firenzes floriner fra 1252. Fra 1386
prægedes den i det tyske område, men med en noget lavere finvægt»). The
electoral archbishops struck gold «fra 1348 med Præg som florentinske
Dukater» (Wilcke 1950, 7-1 p. 70); Wikipedia DE gives the first coinage at
66 to the Cologne mark, 23½ Kt, 3,469 g fine (1354).

### 2.2 The electors' unions and the slide 1385-1464

Wilcke 1950 ch. 7-1, pp. 70-77, is the fullest account we hold (after Jesse,
Noss, Diepenbach). Key moves:

- **26 Nov 1385**, ten-year union of Köln, Trier, Mainz, Pfalz: common gulden
  at 23 Kt, 66/Mk, John the Baptist obverse, Dreipass reverse (p. 70-71).
  Wilcke's judgement: not a gold currency, «højst … en Dobbeltmøntfod» (p. 71).
- 1396 the union lapses; **19 Sep 1399** new ten-year union at 22½ Kt,
  Vierpass reverse; Köln and Trier soon go to 20½, Pfalz to 22 (p. 71).
- Gold did not grow in the electorates — it came in through the **Rhine
  tolls** (Pfalzgrafenstein, Stolzenfels, Ehrenfels): the gulden is a toll
  coin before it is a trade coin (pp. 71-72, 77).
- **23 Jun 1402** King Ruprecht's Münzgesetz at a Reichstag in Mainz restores
  22½ Kt / 66 — Wilcke: Ruprecht's aim was «at gøre den rhinske Gylden til en
  værdifast Rigsmønt» (p. 73); enforced by the cities' threat, Boppard 5 Mar 1404.
- **15 Aug 1409** 22 Kt / 66; **1417** 20 Kt / 66 (without Köln);
  **20 Mar 1419** 19 Kt / 66⅔ (p. 74).
- **29 Oct 1454**: publicly unchanged, but a secret deed of the same date
  releases the lords from striking and lets a mint, if it must, strike 68 at
  19 Kt — «Dette Aktstykke maatte i hvert Fald holdes hemmeligt!» (pp. 74-75).
- **11 Oct 1464** 68⅔ at 19 Kt (p. 76).

### 2.3 Postulatsgulden — the debased half-gulden

Köln's archbishops struck «Postulatsgulden» secretly at Deutz (from ~1458)
and Rheinberg (1475), imitating Utrecht's (Rudolf von Diepholz 1426-31);
Wilcke's table line: «Postulatsgylden = halv rhinsk Gylden» (p. 77). Horn
gulden of Liège were «de sletteste knapt 10 Karats nederlandske Gylden»
(p. 43 area, finds list). Both reached Denmark (§4.1). Out of scope as
coins; relevant because they are why Danish accounts distinguish «rhinske
Gylden, gode og onde».

### 2.4 The north-German divergence: Lübeck vs Hamburg/Lüneburg

- **Lübeck** struck florins under a privilege of **1340**, florentine weight
  and fineness, 66/Mk ≈ 3,537 g; «I Aarene 1343-71 skal der … være udmøntet
  ialt 635 000 Stkr.» Lübeck held 23⅓ Kt and drifted only in weight (68, 69,
  70 per mark) — «hvorved de lybske Guldmønter forblev i Forbindelse med de
  ungarske og italienske Dukater» (Wilcke 7-1 p. 79). → ducat lineage, not ours.
- **Lüneburg 1434, Hamburg 1435**: imperial privileges to strike Rhenish
  gulden «af Skrot og Korn som Kejserens og de rhinske Kurfyrsters, altsaa
  efter 1454 68 Stkr. paa den 19 Karat fine M» (p. 80). This is the first
  north-German adoption of the Rhenish standard proper — Hamburg's gulden is
  the standard's nearest approach to Denmark before 1496.
- Wendish recess of **1410** (Lübeck, Hamburg, Wismar, Lüneburg) tariffs gold
  as legal tender alongside silver: English noble 35 ß, Lübeck gulden 16 ß,
  «Den Ryneschen ghulden vor XIII ß», Gelderland gulden 7 ß (p. 80) — the
  Lübeck gulden 3 ß above the Rhenish, «i Overensstemmelse med dens bedre
  Gehalt» (p. 81). Recess of **1450** forbids trading «by golde», only in
  Lübeck marks, but debts over 20 Mk are ⅔ payable in gold or coarse silver.
- Lübeck public decisions cite Rhenish gulden 19× in 1417-26 against one in
  Lübeck gulden (p. 82). Lübeck, Hamburg (nettle leaf) and Lüneburg (lion)
  **countermarked** foreign gulden «som gænge og gæve» — the «stemplede
  Gylden» of Danish accounts (p. 81-82).

Wilcke's summary of the 15th century: «den rhinske Gylden regerede … over
Tyskland og Norden, indtil Amerikas Opdagelse og den rige Tilgang af Sølv»
(p. 81).

### 2.5 The 1490 norm and its imperial adoption

Electors' agreement **15 Nov 1490** (in force 2 Feb 1491): 71⅓ per mark,
18½ Kt, alloy 3½ Kt silver + 2 Kt copper, Salvator obverse, legend MONETA
AVREA RENENSIS with year (Wilcke 7-1 pp. 76-77). The German text
(Wilcke 6, after Hirsch VII p. 49-50): «Der iglicher halte nuynzehend halben
grat fyns golts sonder alle remedium … hundert und sieben, und nit mehr uff
anderthalb Collnisch marcke». Adopted at **Worms 1495**, confirmed Lindau
1497, Freiburg 1498; Bamberg/Würzburg/Brandenburg 1503 and 1506, Hessen 1509;
the Schwabach Wardein's bestallung 1503; Saxony, Austria and cities struck to
it; Kaisertag Frankfurt Sep 1509 (Wilcke 6). Wilcke: «Der kan saaledes ikke
herske nogen Tvivl om, at denne Møntfod var almindelig kendt over hele
Tyskland og ikke vel kunde være ukendt i Danmark.»

**Gold:silver ratio** moves from 1:10,761 to 1:11,179 over 1385-1491 (7-1
p. 77) — the slide is gold becoming dearer, not just lords cheating.

## 3. German track II — 1491-1625 (codification, the Esslingen break, Ferdinand)

### 3.1 The Sølvgylden — silver built to equal the gold gulden

Sigismund of Tirol's Hall Guldiner (1486) was made to «ækvivalere den saa
meget huserende rhinske Guldgylden» (Wilcke 7-1 p. 33); the Joachimsthaler
(1518) follows. The Guldengroschen is literally «den store Groschen … ved at
svare i Værdi til en Guldgylden» (Wilcke 1919 p. ~24). This is why Danish
tariffs equate «rhinsk Gylden eller Jochimsdaler» (§5.4).

### 3.2 Esslingen 1524 — the break the Danish prose omits

Karl V's Reichsmünzordnung of Esslingen, **10 Nov 1524**, §§ 49-50: a gulden
«af Korn og Skrot 89 Stkr. paa den 22 Karat fine Mark cölnsk», because «die
Notturift ervordert» — gold scarcity and the export of the better coin
«der niemand nützlich noch fürtreglich ist, dann frembden Nation vnd
Auslendern» (Wilcke 6; Wilcke 1919 p. ~24 gives the date 10 November 1524 and
«Guldgyldenen til 89 Stkr. af 1 Mark 22 Karats Guld, et Sølvstykke = en
Guldgylden til 8 Stkr. af den 15 lødige Mark»). Wilcke's table: 89 / 22 Kt /
2,628 g rauh / 2,409 g fein — **almost exactly the Danish 1514 fine weight
(2,437) on a different alloy**. Wilcke 6: «Denne nye Møntfod fra 1524 kunde
visselig ikke tjene Kong Hans til Undskyldning».

Consequence for our prose: the sentence «the imperial norm stood on the 1490
form until Karl V kept it in 1551» is wrong in its middle — 1524-1551 the
imperial LAW prescribed 89/22 Kt, and Karl V's 1551 ordinance RESTORED the
1490 form. (The note to `wikipedia-de-reichsmuenzordnungen-gold`, used on the
Reichsdukatenfuß card, says Esslingen 1524 «erklärt allein die Cöllnische
Marck zum allgemeinen Müntzgewicht» — the gulden clause is not in that ref;
Wilcke is the source for it.)

### 3.3 The Valvationstag of Nürnberg, 1551 — assay, not law

Reichstag Augsburg **14 Feb 1551** sends 2 councillors + 1 Wardein per Kreis
to Nürnberg «til Fastsættelse af Værdien af en Række i Landet kurserende
Mønter. De skulde prøve hver enkelt Mønt … samt oplyse dens Vægt, Gehalt og
det Antal Kreuzer» (Wilcke 6). Findings relevant here:

- Rhenish **Normalgulden in practice**: «som oftest kun udmøntedes 108 Stkr.
  af 1½ cølnsk Mark 18⅓ Karat fint Guld» = 72 at 18⅓ — Wilcke's table row
  «Rhinsk Gylden 1524-51 · 72 · 18⅓ · 3,249 · 2,481».
- Danish silver: «Schwedisch, Denmärkisch, Polnisch … seind dermassen am
  Schrot und Korn … ungleich befunden, das denen ain gewiser werth nit zu
  sezen» — refused as payment.
- Danish gold, two valuations (§6.1): two-bar Hans type 69½ Kreuzer at
  «17 Karat 9 Gren»; star/nettle-leaf Hans type 67 Kreuzer at 17 Kt;
  Christian III's Schleswig St.-Andreas gulden «noget bedre estimeret, nemlig
  til en Finhed af 18 Karat og en Værdi af 70 1/2 Kreuzer».

### 3.4 Karl V, 28 July 1551

Augsburg Reichsmünzordnung: Thaler 68 Kr, 24 ß lübisch 60 Kr, «en rhinsk
Guldgylden (= Sølvgylden) til 72 Kreuzer»; gulden kept «i den af de 4
Rhinfyrster oprindelig vedtagne Skikkelse» — 107 from 1½ Mk at 18½
(Wilcke 6, after Hirsch I p. 354). The 1551 silver gulden (Guldengroschen at
72 Kr, 7½ per 14 Lot 2 Gren mark) was invented «til at opveje Guldgyldenen»
(Wilcke 7-6 p. ~521).

### 3.5 Ferdinand I, 19 August 1559 — the final imperial form

Wilcke 6: «Ved Kejser Ferdinand I.s Møntedikt af 19. August 1559 fastsattes
endelig Møntens Gehalt til 72 Stkr. af den 18 1/2 Karat fine Mark. Dens
Værdi sattes til 75 Kreuzer eller 1 Thaler (Specie) 7 Kreuzer. Fornævnte
Udmøntningsforhold for den rhinske Gylden blev gældende helt ind i
Christian IV.s Tid.» Same edict: «med 6 Maaneders Varsel saavel
Christian III.s som Kong Hans' Guldgylden, baade med 2 Bjælker og med
Stjerne, forbudte og erklæret ugyldige» (Hirsch I p. 392-93).

Wilcke 7-6 p. 524: in the 1559 order the silver is the cornerstone —
«Sølvguldgyldenen er forladt … Guldfoden er opgivet»; the Reichsgulden of
60 Kr «pretenderer ikke nogen Guldgyldenværdi». The same ordinance admits the
ducat (Reichsdukatenfuß) — the gulden is from here the SECOND imperial gold
coin (Bobzin: ducat 104 : gulden 75 Kreuzer).

Note the 1497 Swedish ordinance and the 1559 edict share one row in Wilcke's
table («Svensk Gylden 1497 og Kejser Ferdinand I.s Gylden af 1559 · 72 ·
18½ · 3,249 · 2,504»): Hans prescribed in 1497 what the Empire decreed in 1559.

### 3.6 After 1559 in Germany

- Saxony struck gulden «omkring Aar 1500: 71 1/3 Stkr.», and ducats after
  1622 (Wilcke 7-6 p. 528).
- Wilcke 1919 (Christian IV's Møntpolitik, Kipper period): «Den rhinske
  Guldgylden blev … betydelig ringere i Finhed end alle øvrige Guldgylden».
- Rheinischer Münzverein strikes the gulden «bis zum Ende des ersten Viertels
  des 17. Jahrhunderts» (Wikipedia DE «Rheinischer Gulden»); 18th-century
  pieces commemorative only (last a Würzburg New-Year gift 1798, per the
  2026-09-05 dossier).
- lex.dk: «Guldindholdet var i 1550 sunket til 2,5 g».

## 4. Danish track I — before any Danish coin (to ~1495)

### 4.1 Foreign Rhenish gulden as Denmark's large coin

The Danish silver system (Lübeck reckoning, introduced 1424 under Queen
Philippa's union with Hamburg, Lübeck, Lüneburg — Wilcke 7-1 p. 69) could not
serve large payments. «Derfor indkom den rhinske Gylden, Guldgyldenen eller
simpelthen Gylden i Danmarks Pengevæsen» — accepted voluntarily at metal
value, until «der sluttelig opstod Gyldenregning, der konkurrerede med
Markregning og … afløstes af Dalerregning» (7-1 pp. 69-70). «Hovedomsætnings­
mønten var den rhinske Gylden fra Rhinbyerne, Cøln, Trier, Mainz og
Frankfurt samt fra Brandenburg og Liibeck, som særlig har kurseret i Danmark
paa Kong Hans' Tid» (p. 70).

Evidence (7-1 pp. 36-43):
- Hoards: dozens of 14th-15th-c. gulden from Köln, Mainz, Trier, Frankfurt,
  Bonn, Nürnberg, Utrecht, Geldern, Lübeck, Hamburg across Denmark and
  Schleswig-Holstein (finds list 1761-1890s).
- Queen Christine's household accounts 1502-05: «331 rhinske Gylden, hver
  Gylden regnet for 2 M» (1503, Ribe/Kolding customs); 3 Rhenish for 7½ M
  against 3 Horn gulden for 3 M; 500 M d. for 200 Rhenish gulden.
- Pledges of crown land in r. g.: 1495 (216 r. g.), 1500, 1502.
- Michel Bleck 23 Feb 1529: «4 rhinske Gylden udi Guld hver saa god som
  halvfjerde M d. 18 rhinske Gylden, gode og onde (!) hver gylden saa god som
  2½ M d og 11 hornske Gylden … 1 M d».

So the standard is present in Denmark as **money of account and payment**
from the 15th century; as **Danish coinage** only from ~1496.

## 5. Danish track II — the royal coinage and its instruments, 1496-1632

### 5.1 Hans (1481-1513): the first Danish gold, no Danish ordinance

- **Coin:** 1 Rhinsk gylden u. år, Malmø or København, two-bar type
  (Galster 27A, Schou 1-6); nettle-leaf type (Galster 27B, Schou 7 — «kendes
  ikke mere»); **½ Rhinsk gylden** u. år (Galster 28, Schou 8, RRR, KMMS);
  Bergen 1 Rhinsk gylden u. år (unique, Dresden; not in Galster/Schou)
  (danskmoent 1rhingyl, halvrhin, norge/hansGej).
- **Date/purpose:** «formentlig slået omkring 1497 for at aflønne de tyske
  lejetropper i krigen mod Sten Sture» (danskmoent hg27 after Zinck).
  Hvitfeldt: «150,000 rhinske Gylden til Toget mod Sverige 1497» (Wilcke 6);
  the cavalry alone cost «15,000 Gylden maanedlig i Sold» (Wilcke 6 n. 23).
- **Norm it imitates:** the imperial 71⅓ / 18½. Wilcke 6: Hans «var den første
  danske Konge, der lod slaa Guldmønt»; the gulden «var holdt i Skrot, men
  ingenlunde … i Korn». Schive weighed it 3,283 g — «paa det nærmeste … 72
  Stkr. af Marken brutto»; hence Wilcke lists Hans under both 71⅓ and 72.
- **Hans' own written norm** is Swedish: Møntanordning of **4 Dec 1497**
  (St. Barbara's day), Stockholm, to mintmaster Michel Johansson, «guldmynt på
  Rhenska gulden slag», 72 at 18½ (Schive) — «Nogen svensk Guldmønt blev dog
  aldrig slaaet» (Wilcke 6; full text Wilcke 7-1 p. 117).
- **Assay** (§3.3): two-bar 17 Kt 9 Gren ≈ 17¾; nettle-leaf 17 Kt. Wilcke
  attributes two-bar → Swedish campaign 1497, nettle-leaf → Ditmarschen 1500,
  both «bestemt til Sold for Krigsfolket og — til Fordel for Kongen — været
  søgt noget forringet i Gehalt». **Hypothesis in Wilcke, not a document** —
  keep «Wilcke relates/attributes» wording.
- The «star» of the German valuation is the Holstein nettle leaf: Museum
  Regium 2nd ed. (Lauerentzen), «stella, seu potius urticæ folium» (Wilcke 6).

### 5.2 Christian II: 1513 and 1514 — the first Danish instruments

- **22 Jul 1513, Haandfæstning**: coin to be struck «saa at thue mark giør
  fyldest for en riksdlr. (af andre Kilder en rhinsk Gylden)» (Wilcke 7-2
  p. 149).
- **1 Aug 1513, Beskikkelsesbrev for Dynes Blicher, Malmø** (Wilcke 7-2
  pp. 149-150): «Tillige skal han mønte guldmønt hele og halve Gylden saa god
  som rinske Gylden, skal han og mønte os hvert aar … 20 M Guld og 20 M Sølv»;
  and «2 danske M pendinge … skal gøre fyldest for en rinsk gylden». Wilcke's
  formula for it (p. 152): «Rhinsk Gylden 71⅓ · 3,278 · 18½ · 770,83 · 2,527»,
  gold:silver 1:11. **This is the FIRST Danish act naming the Rhenish gulden,
  and it prescribes the imperial norm.** It was not executed: «den rinske
  Guldgylden hvoraf intet Exemplar ses bevaret» (p. 152); «Ordningen af 1513
  slet ikke kunde opretholdes, saaledes at Skillingen af 1513 overhovedet ikke
  naaede at komme til Udførelse, ikke heller den rhinske Gylden» (p. 155).
- **Summer 1514, Brev to «Myntemester Dienis udi Malmo»**, «efter Rigsraadets
  Raad og Samtykke» (Wilcke 7-2 p. 153, after Suhm Ny Saml. II 1 p. 141 /
  RA Chr. II Registre 1513-23 fol. 189): Nobel 23½ Kt 16/Mk; **Rhinsk Gylden
  18 Karat fint nobel Guld, 72**; Sølvgylden 14 Lod 8½; … Wilcke on «fint nobel
  Guld»: «Gyldenens Finhed er derfor slet og ret 18 Karat» (p. 153). Gold:
  silver 1:9,9 (p. 154). Wilcke's verdict: «den rinske Guldgylden er forringet
  i Finvægt» (p. 155); the whole ordinance «tværtimod var en betydelig
  Forringelse i Strid med Haandfæstningen» (p. 155) — «et velment Forsøg paa
  at stikke Folk Blaar i Øjnene» (p. 156).
  So the Danish 18 Kt is a **deliberate half-carat undercut** of the imperial
  18½, one year after an act that had prescribed 18½.
- Christian II coinage: none known (danskmoent 1rhingyl lists none).
- **3 Aug 1514** Norway: «Sølv- og Guldvægten skal være ligedan i Norge som i
  Danmark; for Fremtiden skal den cølnske Vægt bruges … ei nogen indføre og
  sælge … andet Guld end det som godt er af Vægt og Værd og som for Fyldest
  gaar og gives i Tyskland» (p. 156, after Paus 3 p. 281-82). 24 Aug 1515
  Sjælland renewal: «24 fi gælde en rhinsk Gylden» (p. 156).

### 5.3 Frederik I (1523-1533): 1524, and the gulden in accounts

- **25 Feb 1524, Rigsraadets Brev** (Wilcke 7-2 pp. 180-183): gold clause
  only «fyn nobell guild … xxiij kaarat … xvj stycker»; silver; **no Rhenish
  gulden.**
- **After 25 Feb 1524, Kongebrev** to Jørgen Kock, Malmø («et lignende fik
  Møntmesteren i Riibe»), p. 183: «i Guld: Nobler (den vegne Mark skal holde
  23½ Karat og skraade 16 Stkr.), **Rinske Gylden (18 Karat, 72 Stkr.)**, og i
  Sølv: Tomarksstykker …». Wilcke's table p. 187: «Rhinsk Gylden … 72 · 18 K. ·
  750 · 3,249 · 2,437». → the 1524 Danish gulden instrument is the
  **Kongebrev**, not «the ordinance of 25 February 1524».
- **Coins:** 1527 Malmø, standing portrait (Galster 59, Schou 1-3;
  danskmoent f1g59: 3,19 g, .750); **1531 / u. år Gottorp**, St. Andrew,
  shields of Holstein, Schleswig, Stormarn, Norway (Galster 122, Schou 1-4;
  f1g122: 3,19 g, .750, 2,40 g fine) — Frederik's ducal-capacity coin.
  Photographs Wilcke 7-2 p. 216.
- **Rates in accounts** (7-2 pp. 220-222): 1527 household «en Gylden til
  27 ß»; 1529 «10 g. = 25 M d.»; 1530 Mogens Gyldenstjerne at Akershus sets
  «en rhinsk Gylden til 3 M d., medens den forhen regnedes kun til 2½ M»
  (Schive p. 155); a sack of 1353 r. g. = 2029 M 8 ß → 1 r. g. = 1½ M lybsk.
  Hanse cities 1506-28 «28 rhinske Gylden lovmæssigt sat til 22 ß pr. Stk.»
  [sic, OCR suspect], trade 26-29 ß; 1529 legal: old 26 ß, new 24 ß; Lübeck
  gulden/ducat 32 ß legal, 36-40 ß in trade (after Jesse p. 218).

### 5.4 Christian III (1534-1559)

- **1534-39 Reynold Junge's account** (CORRECTED 2026-10-10). Primary: Galster
  (udg.), *Reynold Junges Møntmesterregnskaber 1534–1540* (1934), Guldregnskab
  p. 108: «Dar van de ℳ gheweghen holt 18 kraet 4 greyn … gheschrodet up de
  gheweghenn ℳ 72 stucken. Vnde is ym talle 24470 golt gulden» = 72 / 18⅓ Kt,
  24 470 pieces; arithmetic check: fine 261 Mk 10 L 3 Q / alloyed 342 Mk 8 L
  2½ Q = 0,7639. Galster's Indledning p. XVI converts it to «3,19 · 764 · 2,44»
  (the figures danskmoent prints for Galster 130/131 — an account norm, not an
  assay). His Udmøntningsskema (p. 184, verified on the page image) prints
  «17⅓ Karat» — a MISPRINT; Wilcke 1950 (7-3 pp. 242–244, fine 2,345 g) copied
  that row. The earlier version of this dossier called these «Mårten's
  accounts» (Mårten Mynteskrivare's Swedish Svartsjö accounts are cited in the
  same Wilcke table for klippings only) and carried 17⅓ — both wrong.
- Valuations of the 1530s (7-3 p. 239): «En Guldgylden, dansk Guldgylden af
  rhinsk Guld, rhinsk Guldgylden eller stemplet rhinsk Gylden, Joachimsdaler
  eller Daler, gik … for 29 … 30 ß Lybsk»; «en Mark Guld regnedes altid lig 72
  Guldgylden» — 72 as the gold-weight unit of account.
- **1540 Recess** (7-3 p. 251, restated p. 255): «Oc skall en rinsk gyllen
  eller en iacopsdall[er] icke dyrere vesles … end for iij M danske» — the
  first statutory Danish TARIFF of the gulden, and its legal equation with
  the Joachimsthaler.
- **Coins:** u. år (1534) Gottorp, shields/imperial orb (Galster 130, Schou
  1-2, Sieg 22); (15)36 Roskilde, portrait (Galster 131, Schou 1-7, Sieg 23;
  c3g131 .764 — «af Galster fejlagtigt henført til Gottorp»); **(15)46
  Flensborg**, 1 (unique, Hede 14, 3,278 g, .750) and 2 Rhinsk gylden
  (unique, Hede 15, 6,556 g, .750) (danskmoent 1rhingyl, 2rhingyl, Hede c3h15).
- **22 Jan 1547, Flensborg bestalling for Jørgen Kock the younger** (Wilcke
  7-4 pp. 281-282, RA T.K. 160): «Guldgylden skulde slaas efter Kurfyrsternes
  Gylden i Værd, Halt, Schrot og Korn» — the electors' 71⅓ at 18½. Wilcke's
  Flensborg table p. 284: «Guldgylden. 1546 · 71⅓ · 18 K. · 3,278 · 2,458».
  **This is the independent instrument §7.5 of the old dossier was waiting
  for** — the Flensborg 1546 gulden weigh 3,278 / 6,556 g = 71⅓ grid exactly,
  and the 1547 bestalling orders that grid in Schleswig. Caveats: (a) the coin
  (1546, predecessor mintmaster Reynold Junge) precedes the act by a year; (b)
  the assayed fineness (.750) is the Danish 18, not the electors' 18½. Honest
  reading: the Schleswig mint worked to the imperial rough weight under a
  written order to follow the electors; whether its 18-Karat alloy was a
  shortfall or a Danish-law residue is not settled by any source we hold.
- **1550 Sound Toll** (7-4 pp. 292-293): instruction to Sander Leyl 8 Jul 1550
  — «de, der ikke havde Nobler, for hver Nobel betale 3 fuldvægtige rhinske
  Gylden i Stedet for tidligere 2½». Sound-toll accounts «omhandler kun
  Rosenobler og Rhinske Gylden samt undertiden Daler»; last account:
  3 534 rhinske Gylden at 1 Dlr 1 ß (p. 293). **Role:** the gulden is a
  statutory toll-payment coin at Helsingør.
- By 1536-59 in the state accounts gold «ikke længer tjente Omsætningen og
  Pengecirkulationen i selve Landet» — it paid foreign luxuries (Rhenish wine,
  cloth, spices) (7-4 p. 298).
- 1557 «sloges 1557 en Guldgylden» — Kølle gives 8 1/16 at 23 Kt: that is the
  **Ungersk** line (Hede c3h1/c3h2), not ours (7-4 p. 307).
- 1559 state accounts: «Guldgylden regnedes til 2 M lybsk og 3 M 4 ß dansk»
  (7-5 p. 319).

### 5.5 Frederik II (1559-1588): struck to the imperial edict

- No Danish gulden ordinance 1524 → 1602. The fineness Frederik's pieces carry
  (Hede .77 = 18½) is Ferdinand's 1559 norm.
- **Metal and motive, documented:** Henrik Rantzau's relation of 11 Aug 1563:
  a large sum of gold and thalers received from Elector **August of Saxony** —
  «I hans lille Sæk var omtrent 10 000 Guldgylden, som er gode. De kunde vel
  ommøntes og leveres og komme til Krigsfolkene for 36 ß Stkt.»; Frederik's
  quittance 18 Aug: 65 000 Gylden + 26 875 Thaler (Wilcke 7-5 pp. 320-321).
  28 Sep 1563 (Halmstad): the King's goldsmith on Holmen has «Kroneguld og
  rinsk Guld … under Hænder for at mønte», to be distinguished from the Hungarian
  gold already struck, one side «et F med en Krone paa» (p. 326).
- **Outturn 1563-64** (7-5 p. 320): Hans de Wüllers 2 259 + Hans Køppelin 995 =
  **3 254 Guldgylden Klippinge**; beside 16 903 Hungarian and 5 460 Krone
  Klippinge. War: the Nordic Seven Years' War.
- Coins: (15)63 København, crowned F, «I GOLT GULDE 63» (Hede 3, Schou 3,
  Sieg 23, 3,248 g, .77); 1564 square klippe, «I GOLT GILDEN 1564» (Hede 6,
  Sieg 24, same specs); 1584 Frederiksborg, Queen Sophie's gift set, unique
  (Hede 7G, Schou 6, Sieg 29, 3,27 g, fineness not given).
- Cabinet weights (7-5 p. 321): 1564 gulden 3,11 g; Hans' and Christian III's
  ~3,27 g; Wismar 1558 3,2 g; Köln ~1400 3,35 g.
- 1568: Rhenish gulden 2 Mk 3 ß lybsk, «stemplet Gylden» 2 Mk 4 ß (p. 321).

### 5.6 Christian IV (1588-1648): legal exit, wartime revival

- **8 Sep 1602 Forordning**: gold schedule = Portugaløser (6¾, 23½ Kt) +
  Ungersk Gylden (67, 23⅓ Kt); Rhinsk Gylden absent (`dk_dukat_portugaloeser_fod.md`
  §3.1). Argument from silence of a comprehensive schedule — strong, but not an
  express ban.
- **1624-1632 coinage** (Harck, danskmoent/harck/c4guld): accounts 1624
  36 506 Rdl, 1627 2 710, 1632 3 212, at «1 Rhinsk Gylden regnedes for 1,25
  Rigsdaler» → 29 205 + 2 168 + 2 570 = **33 942 pieces**. Dated 1625 (most
  common), 1627, 1628, 1632 (Hede 29, Sieg 142, 3,249 g, .76). Harck: 1625
  pieces «fremdateret … med henblik på de forestående krigsudgifter»; 1627-28
  «oplagt slået til anvendelse i forbindelse med trediveårskrigen»; 1632 «må
  have en ganske anden baggrund» (separate peace 1629).
- Wilcke 1924 (Møntvæsenet 1625-1670, A.1, p. 49): «der var givet Befaling
  til Udmøntning af et stort Antal Guldgylden til Lønning af Krigsfolket»;
  Oversigt p. 55 (Wilcke's own cross-reference «Oversigten p. 55»): «Rhinsk Gylden · 1¼ R. in spe. (7½ Mark) · Jørg. 33-34 · 72 ·
  18¼ · 760,42». 30 Dec 1627: 2 144 Guldgylden to the army in Germany (A.1
  note 4; footnote page not recoverable — section pp. 49-58 only).
  Wilcke 6: Christian IV's gulden «fra Trediveaarskrigen» were, like Hans',
  «bestemt til Sold … søgt noget forringet i Gehalt».
- **Glückstadt**: Begnadigungsbrief for Albert Dionis, 30 May 1627, permits
  «Guldgylden og lybske Skillinger … saaledes som de hidtil sloges i
  København» — «Der kendes ikke glückstadtske Guldgylden fra disse Aar»
  (Wilcke 1924, C.1, pp. 208-217 — single page not determinable). Authorisation
  without known outturn.
- **Wolfenbüttel 1627**: «1 GOLDT GULDEN 1627», unique, monogram obverse (Hede
  Wolfenbüttel tillæg 1, Sieg 191; danskmoent guldgyld; Harck NNUM 1975
  pp. 120-121). Struck during Christian IV's occupation; not yet in our data —
  **open item** (standard membership unverified: no fineness).
- **Tariffs 1630s** (Wilcke 1924, A.2, pp. 58-82 — single page not determinable): Guldgylden 7½ Mark d.; gold:silver
  ratio 12,95 for the gulden (Guldkrone 14,08, ducat 13,19); Forordning 1639
  raises it by ½ Mark (ratio 13,81), reversed Dec 1639; Mogens Kaas 24 Dec 1639:
  «en stor Part falske rhinske Gylden» imported — Rhenish gulden «i paafaldende
  Grad … forfalskede ganske særligt i Holland»; Tage Ottesen: gold from the
  German sea-towns «saa och goldt gyllen eller ringsk gyllen».
- Wilcke 1924, A.3, pp. 82-101 (single page not determinable): the «store Udmøntninger af rhinske Gylden ved Tyskekrigens
  Begyndelse» as evidence gold still served as real payment money.

## 10. Target grid and phase targets (kept, corrected)

- **72 is the grid** (`grid_stops: 72`): every Danish decree we hold says 72
  (1514, 1524); even the 1534-39 outturn keeps 72 while the karat drops. The
  imperial 71⅓ (1490-1559, 1551) and the 1513 Danish act and the 1547 Schleswig
  bestalling (both «as the electors'») are the exceptions — see §11 Q2.

    71⅓ / 18½ Karat (2,52707 g fine).

  - from 1559 (Frederik II, Christian IV): Ferdinand's edict 1559, 72 / 18½

## 15. Whose norm, when — Danish vs German track compared (2026-10-08)

Question tested: can the standard be summarised as «the only Danish gold
standard that rested on the imperial norm rather than its own»? Answer: **no**
— both halves fail. Curator-approved conclusion and closing wording below.

| Years | German track (law / practice) | Danish track | Whose norm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1490/91, Worms 1495 | law 71⅓ / 18½ | — | — |
| ~1496–1510 Hans | law 71⅓ / 18½ | no Danish act; weight ≈ 71⅓–72, assay 17¾ (Valvationstag 1551) | Denmark IMITATES the imperial norm, short in fineness |
| 1497 Sweden (not executed) | 71⅓ / 18½ | Hans prescribes 72 / 18½ | Danish-issued act anticipates the imperial 1559 form by 62 years — a parallel, not a borrowing |
| 1513 Blicher (not executed) | 71⅓ / 18½ | «saa god som rinske Gylden» | intent to TAKE the imperial norm |
| 1514 / Kongebrev 1524 | law 1524: 89 / 22 (Esslingen); practice 1524–51: 72 / 18⅓ | **own norm 72 / 18** | piece count = German PRACTICE; fineness own and lower |
| 1527–1536 | practice 72 / 18⅓ | 1536 pieces .764 (18⅓) | de facto the German practice |
| 1546–47 Flensborg (Schleswig) | 71⅓ / 18½ (restored by Karl V 1551) | bestalling «efter Kurfyrsternes Gylden»; coins on 71⅓ weight, .750 | imperial norm ORDERED, Danish fineness struck |
| 1551 / 1559 | Karl V 71⅓ / 18½ → Ferdinand **72 / 18½** | — | — |
| 1563–64, 1584 Frederik II | 72 / 18½ | no Danish act; .77 at 72 | FULLY on the imperial norm |
| 1602 | 72 / 18½ in force | Forordning omits the gulden | Danish law stops naming the standard |
| 1625–32 Christian IV | 72 / 18½; Münzverein ends ~1625 | no act; 72 / 18¼ | nominally imperial (Wilcke: 1559 relations valid «helt ind i Christian IV.s Tid»), actually lower |
| 1619–27 ducal | 72 / 18½ | fineness unknown | undecidable |

Findings:

1. **A Danish norm existed** — Møntordning 1514 (18 Kt), repeated 1524,
   executed by Frederik I and Christian III. «Not its own» is false.
2. **Fully imperial** only under Frederik II 1563–1584 (and the Flensborg order
   1547); Hans and Christian IV only orient to the imperial norm with lower
   fineness.
3. **No reverse borrowing**: no source has the Empire adopt a Danish norm. The
   shared 72 (Denmark 1497/1514; Empire practice to 1551, law 1559) is a
   parallel with German practice, not a Danish contribution.
4. **«Only» fails too**: the ducat is equally an imperial standard; the 1602
   Forordning gives the Ungersk Gylden its own Danish norm (67 / 23⅓) — the
   very act that drops the gulden. **Open**: whether the Danish ducat had a
   Danish act before 1602 (not checked).

**Approved closing (curator 2026-10-08):** «Стопа німецького походження, до
якої Данія то зверталася безпосередньо — Ганс і Фредерік II карбували за
імперською нормою, — то ставила власну, на пів карата нижчу (1514–1547 рр.), а
від 1602 р. перестала приписувати взагалі.» + war-linked coinages, ~¼ Nobel
weight, parallel lines (Ungersk Gylden from 1531, Guldkrone 1563–64). Written
to `data/v2/locations/denmark.yml::fuss_periods.rhinsk_gylden_fod.closing`.
